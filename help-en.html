OneRoadTrip Help: questions and answers

Everything you need to know to find, create, edit and take your road trip with you.

1. Discover OneRoadTrip

What is OneRoadTrip?

OneRoadTrip is a travel itinerary website. You will find more than 1,000 ready-to-go road trips in around a hundred countries, with sights and activities for every stop. Each itinerary can be used as it is or changed to suit you.

Is the site free?

Yes. Viewing, creating and editing itineraries is free.

How does the site make money?

Through bookings. When you book a hotel, an activity or transport through our links, we earn a commission. The price stays the same for you.

Who writes the itineraries?

The itineraries are generated by artificial intelligence, checked several times by other AIs, then checked by a human. The instructions given to the AIs require them to invent nothing and to look for their information on the web. A sight or an activity is only kept if it appears on several websites. The checking AIs then verify every statement. However, the itineraries are not reviewed every day: information that was correct yesterday may be wrong today.

Which languages is the site available in?

In 7 languages: English, French, Spanish, Italian, Dutch, German and Portuguese. A few itineraries also exist in Arabic.

2. Find an itinerary

How do I find a road trip that suits me?

The home page offers five ways to do it:

  • By criteria: you filter by keyword, country, theme, duration or distance. You can show distances in miles and switch the display between photos and maps.
  • Between two points: you enter a departure city, an arrival city and a duration. The site suggests the best stops along the way. You can also give just one city, choose "Round trip" and draw an area: the site then suggests the most beautiful stops in that area. See section 3.
  • From the places: you start from the places you want to see, among those we have mapped. See section 4.
  • World map: you pick an itinerary on the map of the world.
  • Ask Lilia: you describe what you want in one sentence to our assistant, who looks for the closest itineraries. Example: "sunny beaches less than 4 hours from Paris, with the family".

What do Bestseller, ORT tested and Creators mean?

They are three shortcuts placed under the filters:

  • Bestseller: the most viewed itineraries.
  • ORT tested: the itineraries we have travelled ourselves.
  • Creators: itineraries by travel bloggers, signed with their name.

How do I know which season is best?

Each card has a small weather badge. Hover over it, or tap it on mobile, to read "When to go".

3. Between two points

How does the "Between two points" search work?

You enter the departure city, the arrival city and the duration, from 2 to 30 days. The number of stops is optional: leave "Auto" to let us choose. Be sure to pick your cities from the suggested list, otherwise the result may be imprecise.

Can I set the length of the drives?

Yes, in "Advanced options". You set a maximum number of kilometres per day (200 by default) and the detour you accept (50 km by default).

I want to come back to my starting point, how do I do that?

Tick "Round trip (return to start)". The arrival city disappears, as it is no longer needed.

Can I limit the search to a specific area?

Yes, in Round trip mode. First enter your departure city, then tick "Round trip": a map opens, centred on that city. Draw the area to explore with your finger or the mouse, then confirm. If you have no fixed idea, choose "Let us decide".

My area is small, what happens?

If it contains too few places, the site widens it or adds a few stops outside it to complete the loop. It tells you so.

4. Create your own itinerary

Can I build an itinerary from scratch?

oneroadtrip.com/carte_builder.html?mode=expert

Yes, with "From the places". You add your stops one by one, either through the search or by clicking on the map. We connect the points and calculate the route. At the top left, you see live the number of stops, days, kilometres and driving hours.

Can I choose how long to spend at each stop?

Yes. Each stop in the list has its own duration setting, in half days. You can also change the order of the stops or delete one.

Can I add a place that does not exist on the site?

Yes. Click on the map at the spot you want, even if the place is not listed. You can also type its name and choose from the suggestions.

What do the colours of the places on the map mean?

They show how interesting the place is: Must-see, Recommended, Worth discovering or Standard. Places already in your itinerary appear as "Selected".

How do I plan several nights in the same place?

Select that place again in your list of stops. The route will take your return trips into account.

What happens when I confirm?

Your itinerary opens in the advanced editor or in the travel journal, as you choose. There you can edit all the content, add your hotels and bookings, and get a travel journal.

5. Edit, save, book

Can I edit an itinerary suggested by the site?

Yes. All itineraries are fully editable: stops, duration, sights, activities. From an itinerary page, you have three ways to do it:

  • Edit on the map: you rearrange the stops, remove some or add some.
  • Merge: you combine the itinerary with a neighbouring one for a longer trip.
  • Edit everything: you move to the advanced editor to also change the sights and activities, import your bookings and produce a printable journal.

Do I need to create an account?

No to view and edit an itinerary. Yes to:

  • save an itinerary, original or edited;
  • share it;
  • save your bookings;
  • ask for an AI summary.

The account is free. You can sign in with Google or with an email address.

Where do I find an itinerary I have saved?

In "Your trips", your dashboard (oneroadtrip.com/dashboard_user.html). For each trip, you can:

  • open the travel journal;
  • open the advanced editor;
  • edit it on the map;
  • rename it;
  • choose its photos;
  • share it on social networks;
  • delete it.

To send a link to your travel companions, use the advanced editor, "Share" button.

Can I print my itinerary?

Yes. For a proper print layout, open the advanced editor on a computer ("Edit everything" button) then click "Print" in the top bar.

Can I take it with me without a connection?

Yes. You can view your trip offline, provided you have installed the app on your phone. It does not go through the App Store or Google Play: it installs from the site, in a few seconds.

How do I install the app on Android?

Open oneroadtrip.com in Chrome. Tap the site's "Install app" button. If it does not appear, open the Chrome menu (the three dots at the top right) and choose "Install app" or "Add to Home screen". Confirm: the OneRoadTrip icon appears on your home screen.

How do I install the app on iPhone or iPad?

Open oneroadtrip.com in Safari. Tap the Share button (the square with an arrow pointing up), then "Add to Home Screen", then "Add". The OneRoadTrip icon appears on your home screen.

What should I do before leaving without a network?

Open your trip once on your phone while you still have a network. It then stays readable without a connection. Photos of places and parts of the map you have not yet displayed need a network.

Can I share my itinerary with my travel companions?

Yes. Save your trip first, then click "Share" in the advanced editor and send the link that is generated. Your companions see the trip in read-only mode. Be careful: anyone who receives this link can see your trip.

How do I book my hotels and activities?

Each stop offers links to our booking partners. Booking and payment take place on the partner's site, not on OneRoadTrip. We work with recognised partners such as Booking, Trip.com (Skyscanner), QEEQ, Viator (Tripadvisor), CamperDays and many others. By going through our links, you pay nothing more than on their site, and you allow us to receive a small percentage of your purchase.

I have booked, can I add my bookings?

Yes, in the advanced editor, with the "Bookings" button. In three steps:

  1. Choose the type: "Stop" for a hotel, an activity or a sight, "Trip" for a flight, a car or insurance.
  2. Paste the confirmation email you received.
  3. Click "Analyse". Our AI extracts the main points and attaches them to the trip or to the right stop. You can also enter everything by hand.

The AI recognises seven types of booking: flight, car rental, insurance, accommodation, activity, sight and show. It picks up the dates, times, address, reference, price and number of travellers. For a flight, it details each leg, stopovers included. For a car, it notes the place and time of pick-up and return.

You need to be signed in. You can have 20 bookings analysed per month. Always check the result before confirming.

Where can I see all my bookings?

In "Bookings". They are sorted into two blocks: those for the trip (flights, car) then those for each stop, with the date, reference, address and price.

Can I know my budget?

Yes. In the advanced editor, each booking you add goes into the budget. You see the total, the share for transport and accommodation, then the detail line by line. You can add your own expenses.

A problem with a booking, who do I contact?

The partner you booked with. They hold your booking and your payment.

6. Reliability and contact

Is the information reliable?

The AIs that write our itineraries are instructed to invent nothing and to look for their information on the web. A sight or an activity is only kept if it appears on several websites. Other AIs then verify every statement, and a human checks the itinerary. But we do not know every destination by heart, and the itineraries are not reviewed every day: information that was correct yesterday may be wrong today. Opening hours, prices and access conditions can change: check them before you leave, especially for very popular sites.

I have spotted a mistake, how do I report it?

Four options:

  • on an itinerary page, click the red "!" and describe the mistake. The one next to the title is for the whole itinerary, the one on a stop is for that stop;
  • in the advanced editor, open "Tools" then "Report an error";
  • on this help page, click the red "!" at the bottom right;
  • use the "Contact us" form at the bottom of every page, or write to contact@oneroadtrip.com.

I run a hotel, a tourist office or a blog, can I work with you?

We consider every proposal. Write to us at contact@oneroadtrip.com.

7. How it works

How are the ratings of places given?

Each place gets a rating out of 10, which you see as stars. It does not come from a personal opinion. It is calculated in three stages.

  1. What is there on site. For a town or a village, we count around the place, on the free OpenStreetMap map: tourist sites, historic heritage and accommodation. We add its presence on Wikipedia and the number of sights we offer there. A UNESCO-listed site, a nature park or a major monument nearby gives a bonus. For a monument, a beach or a natural site, we start from the rating left by visitors on Google.
  2. The comparison. A place is ranked against its neighbours, against the places in its country and against the whole world. The neighbours count a little more: a beautiful village therefore keeps a good rating, even far from the big capitals.
  3. An AI's opinion on the tourist interest of the place. It weighs more or less depending on whether the AI is sure of itself or not, never more than half of the rating.

What do Must-see, Recommended, Worth discovering and Standard mean?

They are four levels drawn from the rating of the place:

  • Must-see: 8.6 out of 10 and above;
  • Recommended: from 7 to 8.5;
  • Worth discovering: from 4.5 to 6.9;
  • Standard: below 4.5.

They help you spot at a glance, on the map, what is worth the detour.

How are distances and driving times calculated?

In two stages: a calculation, then a check.

  • The calculation. Each journey by car or on foot goes through a road route planner, like the one in a sat nav.
  • The check. An absurd result is rejected: a distance shorter than the straight line, a detour of more than three times the straight line, an impossible average speed (for a car, less than 20 km/h or more than 110 km/h). An AI then re-examines the journey and decides: it is a track, a ferry, a train, a flight. Its answer is checked in turn.

Tracks, boat crossings, trains and flights do not go through the road route planner.

A final check blocks the publication of an itinerary as long as one of its journeys remains inconsistent.

Does the time for a flight, a ferry or a train include waiting?

Yes. To the travel time, we add a fixed waiting time:

  • 3 hours for a flight;
  • 2 hours for a ferry or a boat, 30 minutes if the crossing is less than 30 km;
  • 1 hour for a train, 10 minutes for a metro or a tram in town.

Nothing is added for the car or for walking.

Are the driving times reliable?

They are driving times, without breaks, without traffic jams and without looking for parking. Allow some margin, especially in the mountains and in town. When you edit an itinerary, distances and times are recalculated straight away.

How is the recommended number of nights set?

Each stop has a recommended visiting time. An AI suggests where to sleep each evening and how many nights to spend there. Doubtful cases are reviewed by a human. Nights are counted where you sleep: a short stop, visited in passing, gets none. The number of nights in an itinerary may differ from the time we recommend for the place: for example, we recommend five nights in Paris, but Paris may be just a passing stop in an itinerary. You can change the number of nights for each stop.

What is the "recommended base"?

It is the place where we advise you to sleep after the stop, with the distance to get there. It is always a place in your itinerary. For a short stop, it is usually the next town on the route, so that you do not go back. We avoid making you sleep in a place with no accommodation: a waterfall, a cave, a mountain pass.

Where do the weather and the best time to go come from?

They are written by an AI when the itinerary is created, then reviewed with the rest. They are seasonal trends, not a forecast: check the day's weather before you leave.

How is the carbon score calculated?

We start from the kilometres of the trip and its duration. This gives kilos of CO2 per day, converted into a grade from A+ to E: A+ for less than 5 kg per day, E above 55 kg.

Two badges appear next to the title: the first for the trip by car, the second for the same trip by public transport. You can see what you gain by leaving the car behind.

How does Lilia choose her itineraries?

In three stages.

  1. An AI reads your sentence and draws your criteria from it: departure city, maximum travel time, places or monuments to see, month, wish for sun or snow, type of travellers, themes, length of stay.
  2. The site sorts the whole catalogue according to these criteria, without AI: distance from your city, themes, duration. If you name a specific place, it comes before everything else.
  3. The AI ranks the best results and explains in one sentence why each one fits.

Lilia only searches our catalogue and invents no itinerary. If your request is hard to meet, for example swimming in February near your home, she tells you and suggests what comes closest.

What is the AI summary for?

It is an AI's opinion on your trip, as you have edited it. It contains:

  • up to three alerts: a journey that is too long, a place closed in season, a pointless detour, a visa to arrange;
  • an opinion in three lines: the strong points, the weak points, and a verdict (who it is for, whether to shorten or lengthen, a piece of advice);
  • for each stop: the essentials to see, then the journey to the next stop.

The days, places and journeys come from your itinerary, not from the AI: it cannot change them.

You need to be signed in to generate it. You can then copy it, print it or send it.

You are entitled to one summary per month and per account.
