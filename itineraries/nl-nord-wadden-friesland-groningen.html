Den Helder

🛏️ Suggested base : Den Helder
★★★☆☆
1

A grayish military port at first glance, but the former Willemsoord naval yard hides one of the country's liveliest museums. The Marinemuseum takes you aboard the submarine Tonijn (78 m, in service until 1991), past the bunks, torpedoes, and periscopes. On clear days, they say you can spot Texel through the eyepieces. Open daily except Mondays outside school holidays.

⏱ 120 min
2

Sailors simply call it 'De Werf'. The minelayer Abraham Crijnssen is moored outdoors - step onto the deck at no extra charge. The museum traces the history of the Royal Netherlands Navy since the 17th century. Allow an extra 1 h 30 if you visit the entire outdoor site.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Board the submarine Tonijn: bunks stacked three levels high, ballast valves, and a 6 m² galley make you understand why crews stayed 6 weeks submerged. The Navy Games let kids test navigation simulators. Combined museum + submarine ticket recommended.

Texel

🛏️ Suggested base : Texel
★★★★☆
1

The salty wind greets you as soon as you reach the ferry gangway. Texel is the largest Dutch Wadden island, and Ecomare is its heart: a seal sanctuary, nature museum, and starting point for hikes into Texel Dunes National Park. Injured seals are cared for here before being released into the North Sea. Arrive for feeding times around 11am or 3pm.

⏱ 90 min
2

Ecomare is more than an aquarium: seals, porpoises, and injured birds share open pools. Entry includes direct access to the national park dunes. Adult admission around €13, children €9. Closed Mondays off-season.

⏱ 90 min
3

A third of Texel's surface is nature reserve, and De Slufter is its centerpiece. This polder, abandoned in the 19th century, was gradually reclaimed by the sea, creating one of Northern Europe's rarest brackish ecosystems. Visitors on foot only; dogs on leash.

⏱ 90 min
4

Texel's lighthouse, built in 1864 in De Cocksdorp, stands 35 m tall at the island's northern tip. Plan a return trip from the town where you are staying on Texel to reach the lighthouse. Built of red brick, it overlooks the dunes and the Wadden Sea. Climb its 153 steps for views of the sandbanks and neighbouring islands. Open in season; check opening times on Ecomare's website.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Cycle to De Slufter, the only marsh with tidal channels on the Dutch side of the North Sea. The rental shop near the Markt in Den Burg offers e-bikes. The complete island loop by bike is 54 km—allow a full day with breaks.

⏱ 180 min

If you love birds, the De Muy sector shelters a spoonbill colony closely watched by local naturalists. A 2-hour guided walk from Ecomare (around €7.50) takes you into the heart of the dunes with an English- or Dutch-speaking ranger.

⏱ 120 min

Rent a kayak at Oudeschild to paddle along Texel's east coast from the fishing harbor. The harbor is the island's only active fishing port; auctions happen weekday mornings. Return for lunch on-site: fresh gray shrimp cost less here than elsewhere on the island.

⏱ 90 min

Kornwerderzand

🛏️ Suggested base : Hindeloopen · 15 km
★★★★☆
1

32 km of dyke inaugurated in 1932: the inland Zuiderzee became the IJsselmeer, and two provinces were connected. Engineer Cornelis Lely drew up the plans in 1891 and died in 1929, three years before his work was completed. At Kornwerderzand, view the dyke and the locks between the IJsselmeer and the Wadden Sea. The Vlietermonument, with its observation tower designed by architect Dudok, is closed to the public during the works: do not plan to climb the tower.

⏱ 45 min
2

According to Dutch historical sources, a few hundred soldiers held Kornwerderzand against a German cavalry division in May 1940, and the Blitzkrieg stopped here. Kornwerderzand remains the only defensive position where German troops were repelled during the May 1940 invasion. The 17 original casemates can be visited at the Kazemattenmuseum. Entry included in the Atlantikwall pass.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Visit the Kazemattenmuseum Kornwerderzand: the bunkers and uniforms date from the period, and the original lock control panel is still in place. The adjacent Afsluitdijk Wadden Center opened in 2018 to put the dike's future in the context of rising water levels. Allow 1.5 hours for both sites.

Hindeloopen

🛏️ Suggested base : Hindeloopen
★★★☆☆
1

Painted gables reflect their colors in the canals—here, every house tells the story of an old merchant fleet. Hindeloopen (Hylpen in Frisian) has 875 residents and once owned 100 ships flying its flag. The concentric streets and wooden bridges remain intact.

⏱ 45 min
2

Museum Hindeloopen documents three centuries of local painting on wood, Hindeloopen's traditional costume and maritime trade. The Schaatsmuseum holds the world's largest collection of ice skates and traces the Elfstedentocht, the legendary 200 km race on Friesland's canals. A coffee or tea is offered at the end of the visit.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Stroll across the little wooden bridges: the town is so small you can walk all around it in 30 minutes. On clear days, the promenade along the IJsselmeer lake dike offers views as far as the flat horizon over the water. Good fish restaurants around the harbor.

Franeker

🛏️ Suggested base : Leeuwarden · 17 km
★★★★☆
1

A wool carder built the world's oldest working planetarium between 1774 and 1781—in his living room. Eise Eisinga wanted to reassure his neighbors after a preacher prophesied the end of the world due to a planetary conjunction. His wooden gear mechanism still runs with perfect accuracy. Listed as UNESCO World Heritage in 2023.

⏱ 75 min
2

Museum Martena occupies the Martenahuis, a 17th-century patrician mansion. The town of Franeker has marked dozens of facades with QR codes telling its history—Franeker was once the Netherlands' second university hub, and its amateur scholars worked directly with professors. Free entry to the adjacent church.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Hunt for QR codes on Franeker's facades: each plaque links to an audio anecdote about the building's history. The walk takes 45 minutes and reveals unexpected interior courtyards. Good alternative if crowds at the planetarium are too heavy.

Leeuwarden

🛏️ Suggested base : Leeuwarden
★★★★☆
1

The Oldehove is the North's Tower of Pisa: construction began in 1529 and the ground gave way before work was finished. Architect Jacob van Aken never managed to straighten it. Today you can climb it for panoramic views over Leeuwarden and the polders. Entry €3.50.

⏱ 45 min
2

At the Fries Museum, the scent of old wax from Hindeloopen furniture precedes the halls devoted to Grutte Pier (the 16th-century Frisian warrior), Mata Hari (born here in 1876), and the Vikings. Founded in 1881, the museum is the province's main cultural center. Open Tuesday–Sunday 11am–5pm.

⏱ 90 min
3

Het Princessehof houses the Netherlands' largest collection of Asian ceramics, in an 18th-century palace. The contrast between the classical facade and Chinese and Japanese porcelain is striking. Allow 1 hour; quality souvenir shop.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Climb onto the ramparts of the Blokhuispoort, a former 16th-century prison converted into a cultural center. The building itself is remarkable, and contemporary art exhibitions occupy the cells. Free entry to the building.

⏱ 45 min

If you have an hour to spare, board an electric praamje to cruise along Leeuwarden's canal ring. These small, quiet boats pass beneath low bridges and along the façades of the historic centre.

Holwerd

🛏️ Suggested base : Ameland · 10 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Fishermen say the tide here rises faster than a man can run. The large sculpture on the dike depicting two women waiting for high tide captures the perpetual waiting that sets the rhythm of this coast. Holwerd gives access to the Netherlands' largest salt marsh area: the Noard-Fryslân Bûtendyks.

⏱ 30 min
2

The Noard-Fryslân Bûtendyks, the Netherlands' largest salt marsh area, begins just behind the dike. The Kees Weverswadpost birdwatching blind lets you observe avocets, plovers, and ducks without disturbing them. Free access, on foot from the village.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Take the Wagenborg ferry from Holwerd: the crossing takes 50 minutes and passes sandbanks where seals bask. The regular ferry departs hourly in high season; the fast ferry makes the crossing in 20 minutes for a surcharge. Book online for bikes.

Ameland

🛏️ Suggested base : Ameland
★★★★☆
1

The red-and-white Bornrif lighthouse at Hollum has watched over the coast since 1880 and is one of the few Wadden island lighthouses you can climb. 236 steps take you to a height of 55 m, with views of Terschelling and Schiermonnikoog in good weather. Adult admission around €4.75.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Abraham Fock Museum chronicles sea rescues organized from Ameland for generations. The distinctive local feature: rescuers used horses to launch the lifeboats—a full-scale demonstration takes place regularly in the village of Hollum. Check the calendar before arriving.

⏱ 60 min
3

In the 't Oerd reserve, spoonbills fly over the dunes in tight formation in late afternoon. This ancient dune system in the island's east shelters harriers and mergansers. Accessible on foot or by bike from Buren; marked trails.

⏱ 90 min
4

18th-century shipowner-captains left their houses in the villages of Hollum, Ballum, and Nes—painted gabled facades, carved lintels, gardens enclosed by brick walls. These “commandeurswoningen” are listed as heritage. An interpretive panel in front of each house traces the owner's story.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Cycle from Nes to Hollum along the shell paths, with the sea wind in your face or at your back, depending on your luck. Standard and electric bikes are available to rent at the ferry landing in Nes. The cycle paths avoid the roads and run alongside the dunes.

⏱ 120 min

If the tide is low, join a wadlopen excursion organized by Expeditie Waddenzee: walking on the Wadden Sea floor—a UNESCO area since 2009—toward sandbanks with seals is a strange experience. Excursions from Hollum or Nes, with a mandatory guide.

⏱ 180 min

Each month, the Abraham Fock Museum organizes a demonstration of launching a horse-drawn lifeboat—a technique unique in the world that saved lives for decades. Check the date on the island's website before your stay.

Groningen

🛏️ Suggested base : Groningen
★★★★☆
1

The Groninger Museum is a postmodern building designed by Alessandro Mendini—three pavilions in deliberately clashing styles, set on an artificial island facing the station. Opened in 1994, it houses a collection of over 80,000 objects: Asian porcelain, paintings by the De Ploeg group, and bold temporary exhibitions. The central walkway also serves as a bike path.

⏱ 90 min
2

The Martinitoren has dominated Groningen since the 15th century; locals call it “d'Olle Grieze,” the old gray one. 260 steps for views across the polders to the horizon. The adjacent Martinikerk church houses medieval frescoes discovered in the 20th century.

⏱ 60 min
3

The Forum Groningen smells of a new library and fresh concrete—inaugurated in 2019, this glass-and-wood cube offers a library, a movie theater, the Storyworld museum, and a rooftop with 360° views. Library and rooftop entry is free. Café on the top floor.

⏱ 60 min
4

The Prinsentuin, the prince's garden dating from the 17th century, is one of the few surviving Renaissance court gardens in the Netherlands. It offers a green retreat right in the city centre. Free admission; open during the day.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Walk around the Vismarkt and the Grote Markt at the busiest shopping time: Groningen is a lively student city, with bustling terraces and local markets.

⏱ 60 min

If it's Saturday, the market on the Grote Markt is in full swing between 8am and 5pm: flowers, Groningen cheeses, local vegetables. This is where the city reveals itself in its daily habits, far from museums. Good lunch spot: Globe, in a former cloister behind the Akerk.

Borger

🛏️ Suggested base : Borger
★★★★☆
1

The Netherlands' largest hunebed, D27, measures 22.5 m long and dates back some 5,000 years. The stones, transported from Scandinavia by glaciers, weigh up to 20 tonnes apiece. The Hunebedcentrum, right next door, tells how the first Funnelbeaker culture farmers erected them. Older than the pyramids.

⏱ 60 min
2

In the Oertijdpark, the scent of a wood fire accompanies the reconstruction of an open-air Neolithic village. “Prehistoric men” in period costume demonstrate flint-working and cooking. Open for demonstrations only during Dutch school holidays.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Follow the Hondsrug trails to other hunebeds scattered across the heath: 54 remain in total in Drenthe. Hondsrug Geopark panels guide you through 150,000 years of glacial landscape. The Hunebedcentrum provides hiking maps.

Westerbork

🛏️ Suggested base : Giethoorn · 37 km
★★★★☆
1

Over 100,000 Jews, Sinti, and Roma were deported from Kamp Westerbork between 1942 and 1945 to Auschwitz, Sobibor, Theresienstadt, and Bergen-Belsen. Only 5,000 returned. The Herinneringscentrum Kamp Westerbork places personal stories at the center of its exhibition design: farewell letters, children's drawings, survivor testimonies. Entry €13.50.

⏱ 90 min
2

The 102,000 stones on the former roll-call square each represent a deported victim who never returned. The twisted-rail monument, designed by survivor Ralph Prins, symbolizes transport that came to a halt. The camp site lies 3 km from the museum; the commandant's house dating from 1939, protected under a glass canopy, is the only original building.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Walk to the camp grounds, 3 km from the museum, to see reconstructed barracks and monuments in their original locations. The audio tour (included in admission) lets you hear the voices of former prisoners. A free outdoor guided tour is offered some weekends.

⏱ 60 min

Dwingelderveld

🛏️ Suggested base : Giethoorn · 24 km
★★★★☆
1

In August, heather turns purple as far as the eye can see: Dwingelderveld National Park is Western Europe's largest wet heathland, with 3,700 hectares and over 60 pools. Two flocks of Drenthe heath sheep are led by shepherds to maintain the ecosystem. The park is officially the Netherlands' quietest zone.

⏱ 60 min
2

Officially designated the Netherlands' quietest zone, Dwingelderveld has also become an area where wolves have returned—a presence confirmed in 2024. Visitors must keep a distance of 100 m. Watching migrating cranes from birdwatching blinds in the fall is also popular.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Climb the observation tower near Schaapskooi Ruinen: 360° views over the heath and, on clear days, over the surrounding villages' church spires. Walking and horseback routes covering 57 km of marked trails start at the Dwingelderveld visitor center (Benderse 22, Ruinen).

Giethoorn

🛏️ Suggested base : Giethoorn

Touristy, sure, but once the tour groups leave after 5pm, Giethoorn's canals fall silent again. The village has no roads in its historic center—only 8 km of canals and 176 wooden bridges. The Dorpsgracht is the main artery, to follow on foot or by boat.

⏱ 90 min
2

The village was founded in the 13th century by pilgrims who dug peat, unintentionally excavating today's canals. The 18th–19th-century thatched-roof farms are built on artificial islands connected by individual bridges. Visit the Museumboerderij 't Olde Maat Uus for a reconstruction of early 20th-century rural life.

⏱ 60 min
3

The oars of “fluisterboots” (electric whisper boats) glide silently between the gardens. Moving along the facades at water level reveals details invisible from the towpath. Go early or late in the day to avoid weekend traffic jams on the water.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Rent a whisper boat from one of the Dorpsgracht rental shops: no license required, €15–25/hour. Two hours suffice for the complete circuit. Keep your distance from other boats in narrow passages—the canals are sometimes less than 3 m wide.

⏱ 120 min

If you love quiet, arrive before 9am or stay after 6pm: the tour buses are gone and the village feels lived-in again. The northern part (Giethoorn-Noord) is always less crowded than the center, even in midsummer. Paid parking at P1 and P2 at the village entrance.
