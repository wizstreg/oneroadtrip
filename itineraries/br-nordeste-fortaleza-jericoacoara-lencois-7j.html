Trip info

✨ Highlights▾
  • Jericoacoara was ranked by the Washington Post among the world's 10 most beautiful beaches and only got electricity in 1998
  • Pedra Furada, Jeri's iconic rock arch, can be reached along the beach at low tide or via the Serrote trail.
  • The Parnaíba Delta covers 2,700 km² and contains 75 islands.
  • The Lençóis Maranhenses forms 1,550 km² of white dunes dotted with thousands of freshwater lagoons, fed solely by rain
☀️ Weather▾

Best time: July to December, dry weather with temperatures of 26-31°C and steady winds perfect for Jericoacoara. To see the Lençóis lagoons at their fullest, visit June-September. Avoid February-May: heavy rains, difficult road access, and oppressive humid heat.

📋 Notes▾

Jericoacoara has no direct airport from Europe: you either enter from Fortaleza (4.5 hours drive + 4x4 transfer) or from the small airport in Jijoca 25 km away. The village streets are sand, no pavement, no public lighting—bring a headlamp. The 4x4 crossing between Jeri and Parnaíba only happens at low tide: impossible to improvise, book with a recognized operator (Jeri Off Road, Tropical Adventure). For the Lençóis, the best period is June to September when the lagoons are filled. From January to March, it's dry and far less spectacular.

Fortaleza

Fortaleza, Jericoacoara & Gateway to the Lençóis: 7 Days on Brazil's Northeast Coast : 7 stops

Fortaleza

🛏️ Suggested base : Fortaleza
★★★★☆
1

Fortaleza's Mercado Central is a four-storey cube in the heart of the city, just steps from the cathedral. You climb through it on spiral ramps, and each level has its speciality: leather crafts on the ground floor, colourful hammocks on the first, lace and embroidery on the second, and cachaça and spices at the top. It is touristy but lively, and prices are negotiable. Allow an hour, or longer if a hammock catches your eye — take one dollar per metre as a guideline.

⏱ 75 min
2

The Metropolitan Cathedral, right next to the Mercado, surprises with its sheer scale: 75 meters high, 5,000 seats, Brazil's third-largest church. Inaugurated in 1978 after more than forty years of construction, its neo-Gothic style with Romanesque touches contrasts sharply with the wooden stalls of the neighboring market. Modern stained glass in colored diamond patterns. Quick visit, but the juxtaposition is worth seeing.

⏱ 30 min
3

The Centro Dragão do Mar de Arte e Cultura occupies former port warehouses converted in 1999. It houses the Museu da Cultura Cearense, the Museu de Arte Contemporânea do Ceará, a planetarium and cinemas. The Museu da Cultura Cearense is well worth a stop: it explores sertão culture, the lives of cangaceiros such as Lampião, drought and forró. The surrounding Praia de Iracema district becomes Fortaleza's nightlife hub after dark.

⏱ 90 min
4

Praia do Futuro stretches for 8 km south of the city, lined with barracas—those massive thatched restaurant-pavilions that can accommodate hundreds of people. Chao do Mar, Crocobeach, Itapariká: each has its pool, stage, and vibe. Thursday nights feature the "caranguejada": all-you-can-eat crab while listening to live forró. The beach itself is windier and rawer than the upscale beaches, but it's the real seaside life of Fortaleza.

Activities

Sample Cearense cuisine at a barraca on Praia do Futuro. The absolute specialty is peixada, a fish stew with coconut milk served with pirão (manioc flour puree) and rice. Baião de dois (rice with mung beans and fresh cheese) often accompanies dried meat. To drink: caldo de cana (sugarcane juice) or cajá caipirinha, made with the Northeast's tart fruit.

⏱ 90 min

End the evening on Beira Mar, the long pedestrian avenue along the ocean. The night craft market, open every evening, lines up 600 stalls of hammocks, embroidery, sculptures, and leather goods. You can also grab a quick bite of stuffed tapioca or grilled churrasco. Perfect stroll for digesting.

⏱ 90 min

Canoa Quebrada

🛏️ Suggested base : Canoa Quebrada
★★★☆☆
1

Canoa Quebrada clings to ochre-red cliffs that plunge into the Atlantic. The village lived off fishing until the 1970s, when French hippies heading to India ended up staying. On the cliffs, you'll still find the large crescent moon and star carved during that era, now the village symbol. The main street, nicknamed Broadway, is pedestrian-only and lined with bars, restaurants, and shops. Walk it at sunset when the cliffs turn copper.

⏱ 90 min
2

Praia das Fontes, about 83 km from Canoa Quebrada, a 1-hour 15-minute drive, takes its name from freshwater springs that emerge directly through the cliff. You climb a trail along the ridge, then descend at low tide to watch the water seep from the sandstone. The nearby Mãe d'Água cave once sheltered escaped enslaved people in the 19th century. It is a perfect stop on the road between Fortaleza and Canoa.

⏱ 60 min
3

Aracati, 13 km from Canoa, is worth a 30-minute detour for its listed colonial centre. Blue-and-white Portuguese azulejo façades, the 18th-century Igreja do Rosário and narrow streets leading to the Jaguaribe River await. It is a fine example of Ceará's colonial architecture, and hardly anyone stops here — you will probably have it to yourselves.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Take the classic buggy tour along the cliffs and neighboring beaches. The driver (bugueiro) asks "com ou sem emoção?"—answer "com" if you want the vertiginous sand descents and dune stunts. Three hours round-trip to Lagoa do Coqueiro and Majorlândia beach. Swimming stops, zip-lining over the lagoon, grilled fish lunch. It's the iconic Ceará experience.

⏱ 240 min

If you arrive late afternoon, climb the cliffs on foot south of the village to watch the sunset. Praia do Amor stretches below, deserted. A few jangadas (fishing rafts with triangular sails) return to port, their silhouettes outlined against the horizon. This is Canoa's postcard image.

⏱ 60 min

Jijoca De Jericoacoara

🛏️ Suggested base : Jijoca De Jericoacoara
★★★★☆
1

Jericoacoara didn't get electricity until 1998, and the village deliberately keeps its rustic charm. No paved streets, no streetlights, no neon signs. The streets are deep sand—your shoes go in the bag as soon as you arrive. You can walk the entire village in 15 minutes, between Rua Principal and Rua do Forró. Wooden pousadas, passing donkeys, barefoot kids playing soccer. Drop your bags, stretch out, you've arrived.

⏱ 45 min
2

The Duna do Pôr do Sol is Jeri's obligatory ritual. Every evening, about an hour before sunset, the entire village climbs this 30-meter dune just west of the last houses. You arrive breathless, the sand still warm underfoot, and wait. When the sun touches the Atlantic horizon, everyone applauds. It's cliché, it's touristy, it's also surprisingly moving. Bring a beer and stay after—the sky turns violet for a good 20 minutes.

⏱ 90 min
3

On the opposite side of the village, 3 km to the east, Pedra Furada is an ocean-carved rock arch that has become the symbol of Jeri. At low tide, you can walk there along the beach after checking the tide chart displayed in the village; at high tide, take the Serrote trail. The beach route takes 1 hour and offers views of the Serrote cliffs. It is magical at sunrise, when the light shines through the opening.

Activities

Try kitesurfing if you are there between July and December, the season of steady 25-knot trade winds. Several schools offer beginner packages: 6 hours of lessons spread over 2 to 3 days, about 350 euros all-inclusive. Lessons in French are available. Thermal wind in the afternoon and perfect conditions.

⏱ 180 min

Dine at one of the village's discreet gastronomic spots. Tamarindo, in a candlelit interior courtyard, serves Mediterranean-Cearense cuisine at a surprising level for a village at the end of a dirt track. Sky Restaurant, on a pousada rooftop, makes cocktails with cajá and tamarind facing the sunset. Book ahead, options remain limited.

Jijoca De Jericoacoara

🛏️ Suggested base : Jijoca De Jericoacoara
★★★★☆
1

Lagoa do Paraíso, officially Lagoa de Jijoca, is Jeri's other iconic scene: translucent blue-green water, white sand and floating hammocks set directly in the shallows. You reach it by buggy or 4x4, 45 minutes from the village. Several barracas line the shores — Alchymist is the best-known. Reserve a hammock, order grilled fish and a local kiwi caipirinha, and you will not move for the rest of the day. Water at 28°C, no current and your feet in the sand.

⏱ 240 min
2

Lagoa Azul, smaller and less crowded, sits 5 km from Lagoa do Paraíso. Even clearer water, calmer atmosphere, ideal if you want to escape the groups. Often combined in the same buggy circuit. A few more modest barracas, gentle prices.

⏱ 90 min
3

Mangue Seco is visited on the route west of Jeri. After crossing the Guriú River by ferry, you can take a short canoe trip through the mangroves to observe seahorses, then continue to the trees with aerial roots where swings have been installed. Small crabs, herons and sandy landscapes accompany this stop, usually combined with Tatajuba and Lagoa Torta.

⏱ 90 min

Activities

Do the lagoon circuit by buggy: it's Jeri's classic full-day excursion. Departure around 9 AM, stops at Lagoa do Paraíso, Lagoa Azul, lunch on-site, return via the moving dunes at sunset with a stop at Pedra Furada if the tide permits. Budget 70 to 90 euros for a buggy with driver, split among up to 4 people. Ask for a bugueiro who speaks some English or French.

⏱ 480 min

In the evening, head down to Praia do Forró for "capoeira na praia." Every evening around 8 PM, local capoeiristas set up a roda on the beach around a fire. Berimbau, atabaque drums, singing, and anyone can enter the circle. Not an organized show, no paid entry—it's just the village's nightlife.

⏱ 90 min

Parnaiba

🛏️ Suggested base : Parnaiba
★★☆☆☆
1

The Jeri-Parnaíba crossing by 4x4 is an adventure in itself: 6 hours of dirt tracks, beaches, and river crossings at low tide. You leave Jeri around 7 AM, first stop at Tatajuba (1 hour drive)—a village that was buried by moving dunes in 1990 and rebuilt 5 km away. The old church bell tower still emerges from the sand sometimes. Obligatory photo, swim in nearby Lagoa Torta.

⏱ 90 min
2

You then cross several small ferries to ford the rivers between Tatajuba and Camocim, then follow deserted beaches to Barra Grande, a kitesurf station in Piauí. One-hour lunch stop, swimming. The track then continues through Piauí dunes to Parnaíba, your overnight stop. It's exhausting, bumpy, magnificent. Stay well hydrated, bring a scarf against the sand.

⏱ 240 min
3

Parnaíba itself is a stopover town without great tourist appeal, but Porto dos Tatus, 12 km from the center, deserves an evening look. This is where the lanchas depart for the delta. Small wooden port on stilts, fish shacks, moored jangadas. You can already breathe the atmosphere of tomorrow's adventure.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

If you reach Parnaíba early enough, head to Porto das Barcas, a group of former warehouses beside the Igaraçu River. Walk among the brick buildings and courtyards, then choose one of the restaurants in the area for fish or seafood. Go to bed early — the speedboat for the delta leaves at 8 a.m. the next day, and the crossing has been tiring.

⏱ 90 min

If the fatigue from the crossing still leaves you with energy, swim at sunset at Praia da Pedra do Sal, 15 km from Parnaíba. Fairly wild beach in Piauí state, red cliffs and interior lagoon. Few people even in high season.

⏱ 90 min

Parnaiba

🛏️ Suggested base : Parnaiba
★★☆☆☆
1

The Parnaíba Delta covers 2,700 km² and contains 75 islands in a maze of mangrove-lined waterways straddling Piauí and Maranhão. Depart by lancha, a fast motorboat, at 8 a.m. from Porto dos Tatus for a full day on the water. Stops include watching guarás, scarlet ibises, at sunset, swimming in an inland lagoon and eating grilled fish for lunch on Morro do Meio Island.

⏱ 480 min
2

Ilha do Caju, in the Maranhão part of the delta, is a private 100 km² reserve belonging to the same family since 1847. You'll find about ten different ecosystems and a superb ecolodge for those who want to extend their stay. Group tours don't all stop here—specify if you want to see it, on a private tour. Manatees, armadillos, alligators in the interior rivers.

⏱ 120 min
3

Before returning, operators often make a final stop on the sandbars that emerge at low tide right in the middle of the delta. Several hectares of immaculate white sand that will disappear under water in a few hours. The feeling of being on a floating beach, golden light on the return. Get your camera ready.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Taste the delta's blue crab (caranguejo-uçá) on your return. Several barracas at Porto dos Tatus serve it still live, freshly shelled, with a sauce of coconut milk and cow's milk (the "caranguejo toada"). It's messy, you eat with your fingers, you emerge as happy diners. Count on a good hour.

⏱ 90 min

For those with time before nightfall, go birdwatching in the mangroves at dusk by kayak. Several delta ecolodges organize this outing as a complement to the lancha tour. You glide silently between the aerial roots, and scarlet ibis arrive by the hundreds for the night. One of the trip's most memorable images.

⏱ 120 min

Barreirinhas

🛏️ Suggested base : Barreirinhas
★★★☆☆
1

Barreirinhas is the main gateway to the Lençóis Maranhenses, a 5-hour drive from Parnaíba. Arrival mid-day, quick check-in at the pousada, then first foray into the park in the afternoon. The town lives entirely off park tourism: 4x4 agencies, restaurants, buriti craft shops. Lively main street, but forget the "authentic village" aspect—it's a logistical base.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Lençóis Maranhenses is your trip's visual shock. 1,550 km² of white dunes stretching for 70 km, dotted with thousands of turquoise freshwater lagoons. The sand is so pure it blocks infiltration, and rainwater fills the hollows between dunes—hence the name "sheets" (lençóis) referring to hung laundry. From June to September, the lagoons are full: that's THE moment. Lagoa Azul and Lagoa Bonita are the two most accessible from Barreirinhas.

⏱ 240 min
3

The Preguiças River runs through Barreirinhas and flows down to the ocean. If your day is too short for the dunes, you can take a speedboat trip along the river to Caburé and Mandacaru, two fishing villages between dunes and mangroves. At the 32-metre Mandacaru lighthouse, climb 198 steps for a 360° view over the park. Combine it with the dunes or choose between the two depending on the time available.

Activities

Book a 4x4 with driver to reach Lagoa Azul then Lagoa Bonita late afternoon. The 4x4 drops you at the park edge, then you walk 15 to 20 minutes through the dunes to reach the first lagoon. Water at 28°C, white sand bottom, average depth of 1.5 meters—you can lie on your back without touching. Stay for sunset on the ridges: pink, orange, violet in less than 30 minutes. Unforgettable, and I mean it.

⏱ 240 min

If you're leaving tomorrow, dine at a local restaurant in Barreirinhas. The Maranhense specialty is arroz de cuxá: rice with Amazonian sorrel leaves, dried shrimp, sesame. Strange, dark green, surprising. Restaurant Terral in Barreirinhas makes a good version. You leave with a taste that sums up your Northeast crossing: intense, unexpected, memorable.
