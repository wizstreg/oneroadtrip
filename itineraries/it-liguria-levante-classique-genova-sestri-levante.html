Genoa to Sestri Levante via the Tigullio: 2 Days on the Riviera di Levante : 5 stops

Nervi

Suggested base : Rapallo · 16 km
★★★★☆
1

The Passeggiata Anita Garibaldi is probably the most beautiful seaside promenade in all of Liguria, yet almost nobody mentions it before the Cinque Terre. Two kilometers carved into the cliffside, sheer above the waves, opened in 1862 and named in 1908 after Garibaldi's wife. On mistral days, sea spray shoots over the parapet into your face - pack a windbreaker even in June. On the landward side, stone benches face the open sea, perfect for watching the sunset. Free, open 24/7, and strangely uncrowded on weekdays.

60 min
2

The Parchi di Nervi cover 92,000 m² of romantic gardens beside the sea, formed from three aristocratic estates (Grimaldi-Fassio, Serra and Gropallo) acquired by the municipality between 1927 and 1979. Umbrella pines, palms, century-old magnolias and a 1930s bandstand line the paths. The Festival Internazionale del Balletto stages summer performances here. You may see more local joggers than tourists, which is a good sign. Picnics are allowed, and ice cream vendors appear in season.

90 min
3

The GAM (Modern Art Gallery) occupies Villa Saluzzo Serra, a 16th-century building transformed into neoclassical style in the 19th. Three thousand works total, from Italian Romanticism to the early 20th century: Plinio Nomellini, Rubaldo Merello, Eugenio Baroni. Small, highly digestible, perfect for a rainy late morning. Combine it with the neighboring Wolfsoniana - an American collection donated to the city by Mitchell Wolfson, focused on design and propaganda from 1880-1945, totally off-kilter compared to the rest. Combined ticket €10 for both Nervi museums.

75 min
4

Nervi's Belle Époque villas are worth a detour: Russian and English aristocrats spent their winters here in the late nineteenth century, drawn by the climate and the absence of frost. Villa Gropallo, Villa Serra and Villa Luxoro (now a decorative arts museum, with group visits by reservation) offer pink or ochre façades, Renaissance loggias and Italian gardens. Most are now apartment buildings or municipal museums. There is no official route: stroll along Via Capolungo and Via Marco Sala and look up.

60 min

Activities

If you're staying in Nervi, hop on the regional train to Genoa-Brignole - 12 minutes, €1.80 - and spend the afternoon in the historic center. Via Garibaldi (the UNESCO-listed Palazzi dei Rolli since 2006), Piazza San Lorenzo with its black-and-white striped cathedral, the old port reconverted by Renzo Piano in the '90s. Allow 4-5 hours for a proper tour. Pesto-focaccia meal mandatory, this is where it was invented. Trains Nervi-Brignole every 10-15 minutes during the day, convenient for returning to dine by the sea.

240 min

Swim at Porticciolo di Capolungo, the nicest of Nervi's coves: pebbles, deep water, Italian postcard atmosphere without the noise. Small free beach to the right of the harbor, accessible via stairs from the promenade. Crystal-clear water until October, weather permitting. Bring rubber sandals, the pebbles burn in high summer and are slippery coming out of the water. Small bar at the entrance for granitas, reasonable prices considering the location.

Camogli

Suggested base : Rapallo · 6 km
★★★☆☆
1

Camogli is the postcard you think is kitsch until you see it in person. Houses five to seven stories tall painted in ochre, pink, yellow, lined up like a curtain above the harbor - deliberately painted in vivid colors so fishermen could recognize their house from offshore in all weather. The name Camogli probably comes from "Casa delle mogli" (house of the wives), because the men were at sea for months at a time. Walk along the quay, grab a coffee at pasticceria Revello (since 1964, their chocolate "camoglino" is local and formidable). No sandy beach, just dark pebbles.

60 min
2

The Basilica Santa Maria Assunta has overlooked the harbor since the 12th century, enlarged and rebuilt several times until the 19th. Sober yellow facade, interior pure Ligurian baroque in all its madness: polychrome marble, painted ceiling, crystal chandeliers, gilding everywhere. All because in the 16th century, Camogli was a mini maritime power - its fleet of 700 sailing ships ranked among the largest in Italy in the 19th century. Small crypt with sailors' ex-votos. Free entry, closed noon-3pm, perfect naturally air-conditioned break after the harbor.

30 min
3

San Fruttuoso Abbey is probably the day's highlight, and you have to earn it a bit. Set at the back of a cove between Camogli and Portofino, accessible only on foot (2.5 hours' walk from Camogli) or by boat (30 min, €15 round-trip from Camogli harbor). 10th-century Benedictine monastery, restored and managed by FAI (National Fund for the Environment) since 1983. Short but remarkable visit - Romanesque cloister, Doria tombs carved into rock, small pebble beach out front. Allow €7.50 entry, closed non-holiday Mondays, last ticket 45 minutes before closing.

60 min

Activities

The round-trip to San Fruttuoso by boat (Linea Blu, Golfo Paradiso) is a mini-voyage in itself: 30 minutes' sailing, you follow the cliffside of Portofino regional park, passing Punta Chiappa and Cala dell'Oro (protected zone, boats prohibited). Dolphins sometimes spotted en route. First departure Camogli around 9am, last return around 6pm in high season (April-October). Rate €15 round-trip, tickets at the harbor kiosk - no need to book outside summer. If the sea is rough, the boat doesn't run: have a plan B.

90 min

Divers will have their moment: at 15 meters' depth in San Fruttuoso bay rests the Cristo degli Abissi, a 2.5-meter bronze statue placed there in August 1954 in memory of Duilio Marcante, Italian diving pioneer, and all who perished at sea. Several dive centers in Camogli organize the outing (B&B Diving, ZeroGradi), allow €60-80 with equipment. Also visible snorkeling when the water is very clear - on good days, the statue is discernible from the surface. A copy is displayed in the abbey church.

180 min

Camogli - San Rocco - Punta Chiappa trail, the easy hike that surprises everyone. Start from Camogli harbor, steep 30-minute climb to San Rocco (panoramic church), then gradual descent toward Punta Chiappa, a flat rocky tongue jutting into the sea like a natural pier. Total 1.5 hours one way, 250m elevation gain, trail marked red-white. Swim on arrival, return by boat (10 min, €6) if you've had enough climbing. Avoid noon-3pm in July-August, zero shade on the final section.

Portofino

Suggested base : Rapallo · 5 km
★★★★☆
1

Portofino, let's be clear: it's touristy, it's expensive, and it's still absolutely beautiful. One hundred eighty year-round residents, multiplied by fifty in summer, horseshoe harbor lined with tall ochre and pink houses that have served as backdrop for at least thirty films since the 1950s. The harbor Piazzetta is the village's only real square, twelve cafés lined up where a Negroni costs €22 - fair warning. Park obligatorily in the upper lot (€5/hour!), or come by boat from Santa Margherita (10 min, €5). Coming by car would be masochistic.

60 min
2

Castello Brown dominates the harbor at 70m elevation, a former 16th-century Genoese fort purchased in 1867 by British consul Montagu Yeats Brown - who transformed it into a private residence. Sold to the municipality in 1949, opened to the public since 1961. Two floors of rooms with period furniture, changing temporary exhibitions, and above all a high terrace with plunging views over the bay. Rate €5, closed certain Mondays off-season. Allow 30 minutes for the visit, 15 minutes more to catch your breath on the terrace.

45 min
3

The path to Portofino Lighthouse starts from Castello Brown and continues south for 800m, carved into the cliff above the sea. Allow 20 minutes of easy walking, open views over the Gulf of Tigullio all the way to La Spezia in clear weather. The lighthouse itself isn't visitable - it's an automated building in service since 1917. But the bar-viewpoint just before serves an Aperol spritz for €12 with probably the best view in Liguria. Outrageous and deserved. Avoid midday, the path is exposed full south.

60 min
4

San Giorgio church, above the harbor, is so discreet you easily miss it. First building from the 12th century, rebuilt after 1944 bombing. Inside are preserved relics attributed to Saint George, brought back from the Holy Land by Genoese crusaders in 1098 - hence the name. Small, unadorned, absolute calm after the Piazzetta chaos below. Festa di San Giorgio on April 23, procession, fireworks, probably the only day the village is still Italian and not international.

30 min

Activities

The Portofino Regional Park comprises 1,200 hectares of wooded promontory created in 1935 - one of Italy's oldest protected areas. Over 80 km of marked trails. The classic hike: Portofino - Pietre Strette - San Fruttuoso (2.5 hours one way, 350m elevation gain, red-white trail), return by boat or same path. Forests of holm oak and maritime pine, clear views over both sides of the promontory. Water and serious hiking shoes required, the terrain becomes rocky in places. Free maps at Portofino tourist office.

300 min

If Portofino has wrung out your wallet, Santa Margherita Ligure 5 km to the north is the perfect antidote: elegant seaside resort but 30% cheaper, real neighborhood life behind the palm trees, covered food market every morning (except Sunday). Vittorio Veneto seafront promenade, gelato at Centrale (since 1964), aperitivo on Piazza Caprera. Direct train from Genova-Brignole in 35 minutes - ideal alternative base for those who don't want to sleep in Portofino itself.

180 min

Rapallo

Suggested base : Rapallo
★★★★☆
1

Rapallo's Castello sul Mare is the photo you take first upon arrival: a square bastion literally planted in the water, connected to the promenade by a small jetty. Built in 1551 after a corsair raid by Dragut Reis who had devastated the town the previous year - the castle served as watchtower and refuge for the population. Today, temporary exhibitions inside, but the exterior does the job on its own. Price varies by exhibition, allow €5-8. The postcard photo is at sunset, from the promenade toward the west.

45 min
2

To reach the Sanctuary of Montallegro, take the cable car, inaugurated in 1934. It climbs in 7 minutes from the seafront to an altitude of 612 m, almost vertically, over 2.3 km: quite a ride if heights make you uneasy. Keep looking back at the bay during the ascent as the view opens across the Tigullio. Return ticket €11, departures every 30 minutes, closed noon-2pm outside summer. Check the weather: it closes in strong winds.

30 min
3

The Sanctuary of Montallegro perched at 612m was built starting in 1559 to commemorate a Marian apparition the locals don't joke about: a peasant, Giovanni Chichizola, allegedly saw the Virgin on July 2, 1557, on this promontory. Neo-Renaissance facade redone in 1896, interior overloaded with ex-votos - model boats suspended from the ceiling, marble plaques galore - recounting a century and a half of difficult seas and promises kept. Panoramic view over the entire Gulf of Tigullio from the forecourt. Free entry.

45 min
4

The Basilica Santi Gervasio e Protasio is mentioned as early as 1118, rebuilt several times until the 17th century. Baroque facade from 1857 plastered onto a medieval structure - disjunction visible as soon as you enter. Romanesque bell tower from 1753 separate from the church, leaning very slightly (3°, but visible). Three-nave interior, 19th-century painted ceiling with scenes from the lives of the two titular saints, twin martyrs under Nero. Fifteen minutes suffices, but this is Rapallo's historic heart: the square outside saw the signing of the 1920 Italian-Yugoslav treaty.

30 min
5

Chiavari, 6 km east of Rapallo, is the stop you miss when rushing toward the Cinque Terre. Intact medieval center with its merchant porticos from 1178 - 600m of continuous covered street, one of Italy's oldest for this type of arcade. Former city of the rush-seat chair (the famous "Chiavarina," rush-bottomed chair invented here in 1807, copied everywhere since). Saturday morning market on Piazza Mazzini. More discreet than Rapallo, more authentic than Portofino, and three times cheaper for lunch.

90 min

Activities

Pilgrimage on foot to the Sanctuary of Montallegro for hikers: 2 hours' climb, 615m elevation gain, marked trail starting behind Rapallo station (Salita al Santuario). Historic pilgrims' route, marked by small votive chapels. Descent possible by cable car (€5 one-way) if knees say stop. Trail largely shaded, doable even in July starting at 7am. Water bottle mandatory, no fountain en route before the summit.

240 min

Have an aperitif on the Lungomare Vittorio Veneto, Rapallo's seafront promenade. Three kilometers of palm trees, Liberty-style lampposts and terraces face the bay. The local ritual: a €7-9 spritz at sunset with a board of focaccine and farinata. Reliable addresses include Bar Floriana (since 1929) and Caffè Centrale. Prices tend to fall as you head west away from the castle.

90 min

Sestri Levante

Suggested base : Rapallo · 16 km
★★★☆☆
1

Sestri Levante has the rare fortune of having two bays for the price of one, separated by a promontory of a few hundred meters. The Baia del Silenzio, to the east, is the most beautiful: 200m arc of fine sand, transparent green water, colorful houses in a curtain reflecting in it. Nicknamed thus because no road passes behind, just the backyards of houses - relative silence guaranteed outside July-August. Beach mostly free access, direct from the historic center. Small and therefore packed noon-3pm in summer: aim for early morning.

60 min
2

The Baia delle Favole (Bay of Fables) takes its name from Hans Christian Andersen, the Danish author who came to write here in 1833 - the "Andersen" literary prize is still awarded annually in Sestri in June for children's tales. Beach much longer than its Silenzio neighbor (one kilometer), more exposed to wind, more paid beach establishments (€15-25 for a spot with umbrella). More turquoise water, more present waves - best spot for paddleboarding or windsurfing when there's a breeze. Western side of the promontory.

45 min
3

Sestri Levante's medieval center climbs the headland between the two bays. Narrow paved lanes, somewhat weathered Renaissance palaces and the Basilica of Santa Maria di Nazareth at the top (1604, façade rebuilt in 1834) mark the walk. The former Convento dell'Annunziata, beside the Baia del Silenzio, now hosts conferences and cultural events; you can see its façade as you walk along the bay. The headland is entirely pedestrian.

75 min
4

The Convento dei Cappuccini and its church of Santa Maria Immacolata stand in Sestri Levante on the salita Cappuccini. Walk up to this seventeenth-century religious complex, separate from the Grand Hotel dei Castelli: the church and convent buildings are clear landmarks on the climb. From the headland you can then look out toward the Baia del Silenzio and the Baia delle Favole, without searching for a supposed public access to the hotel's gardens.

30 min

Activities

Hike to Punta Manara, the promontory that closes the Baia delle Favole to the east. Marked trail starting right from downtown (Via Caduti per la Patria), climbs gently through olive trees and maritime pines for 45 minutes, emerges at the ruins of a 16th-century Genoese tower facing the open sea. 180° view over the Gulf of Tigullio, Sestri below, and the Cinque Terre sketched on the horizon in clear weather. Shaded trail, doable in July, 250m elevation gain. Water and sneakers, that's all. Swim on arrival possible in a hidden cove - steep 15-minute descent.

150 min

Tasting of minestrone alla genovese and pansoti in a downtown trattoria - pansoto is that triangular ravioli stuffed with local herbs (preboggion, wild mix of borage, dandelion, nettle, chard), walnut sauce mandatory ("salsa di noci"). Honest addresses: Cantine Cattaneo, Polpo Mario. Allow €12-18 per dish, €30-40 per person with glass of wine. All this in a town where Andersen wrote - not a bad way to finish a Ligurian road trip.

90 min
