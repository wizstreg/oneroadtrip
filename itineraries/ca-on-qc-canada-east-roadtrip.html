Eastern Canada Road Trip: Toronto, Niagara Falls & Québec City : 14 stops

Toronto

🛏️ Suggested base : Toronto
★★★★☆
1

Kensington Market is a former neighborhood of Jewish, Portuguese, and Caribbean immigrants, transformed into an open-air market. Its brightly painted Victorian houses now house thrift stores, international grocery stores, and independent cafés. Nearby, Chinatown stretches along Spadina Avenue with herbal shops, dim sum, and signs in Mandarin—one of North America's largest Chinatowns.

⏱ 90 min
2

Graffiti Alley runs for over a kilometer between Portland Street and Bathurst Street, in the heart of Queen West. Every wall is covered with monumental murals renewed each year. Continue along Queen Street West, Toronto's bohemian thoroughfare, lined with galleries, vinyl record shops, and Canadian designer boutiques.

⏱ 75 min
3

The Distillery District occupies the former Gooderham & Worts distillery, founded in 1832 by two English immigrants. By the 1860s, this operation was producing over two million gallons of whisky annually—the largest distillery in the British Empire. Shuttered in 1990, purchased in 2001, it reopened in 2003 as a pedestrian quarter: more than 40 Victorian red-brick buildings, designated a National Historic Site, now house art galleries, glassblowing studios, and microbreweries.

⏱ 90 min

Toronto

🛏️ Suggested base : Toronto
★★★★☆
1

The CN Tower stands 553 meters tall. Built between 1973 and 1976 by Canadian National to solve radio interference caused by downtown skyscrapers, it was the world's tallest freestanding structure for over 30 years. More than 1,500 workers labored for 40 months. The Sikorsky helicopter “Olga” hoisted the antenna's 39 sections to the summit. The glass floor at 342 meters looks down into empty space. On clear days, the view reaches as far as New York State.

⏱ 90 min
2

Catch the ferry at Jack Layton Terminal (departures every 15 minutes in season) to the Toronto Islands. Ward's Island preserves a cottage village inhabited year-round since 1880, with gardens and shaded pathways. Centre Island offers beaches facing Lake Ontario and unobstructed skyline views. The archipelago forms a 4-kilometer chain of 15 entirely car-free islands.

⏱ 120 min
3

The Royal Ontario Museum, founded in 1912, houses over 13 million objects. Its modern aluminum-and-glass crystal facade, the “Michael Lee-Chin Crystal”, was added in 2007. Its collections of ancient Chinese art, dinosaur fossils, and minerals rank among the world's most important. Alternative: the Art Gallery of Ontario, redesigned by Frank Gehry in 2008, featuring a 180-meter wood-and-glass gallery suspended above the street.

⏱ 120 min

Activities

Evening in Koreatown along Bloor Street West between Christie and Bathurst. Korean barbecue restaurants, karaoke venues, and soju bars line several blocks. Alternative: Little Italy on College Street for its terraces and cafés.

⏱ 60 min

Niagara Falls

🛏️ Suggested base : Niagara Falls
★★★★★
1

Niagara Falls on the Canadian side delivers the most dramatic view of Horseshoe Falls: 57 meters high, roughly 670 meters across the crest line. About 2,800 cubic meters of water per second are permitted to flow over the falls during daytime in tourist season—at night and off-season, up to 75% of the flow is diverted to hydroelectric plants. Approximately 90% of the visible flow passes over Horseshoe Falls. Erosion pushes the falls upstream about 30 centimeters annually. 12,500 years ago, they stood 11 kilometers downstream at Queenston.

⏱ 60 min
2

Journey Behind the Falls: tunnels carved through rock behind the curtain of water lead to two observation platforms at the lower third of Horseshoe Falls. You feel the vibration of the mass of water and the constant mist. A yellow raincoat is provided—it will soon be soaked.

⏱ 45 min
3

Niagara-on-the-Lake, 25 kilometers north of the falls, is a former Loyalist town founded in 1781 by refugees from the American Revolution. Named Newark, it served as the first capital of Upper Canada from 1792 to 1796. Razed by Americans during the War of 1812, it was rebuilt in remarkably preserved Georgian and Victorian style. Its historic core, anchored by Queen Street with old-fashioned shops, has been a National Historic Site since 2004.

Ottawa

🛏️ Suggested base : Ottawa
★★★★☆
1

Parliament Hill overlooks the Ottawa River from a limestone promontory. The current buildings, in Victorian Gothic Revival style, date from 1859. After the fire of 1916, Centre Block was rebuilt and crowned with the Peace Tower, 92.2 meters high, inaugurated in 1927 to commemorate Canadian soldiers who died during the First World War.

⏱ 90 min
2

The Rideau Canal, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, stretches for 202 km between Ottawa and Kingston. Built from 1826 to 1832 under Lieutenant Colonel John By, it was intended to provide an alternative military route after the War of 1812 in case of an American attack. More than 6,000 workers, mainly Irish immigrants and French Canadians, dug the canal—around 1,000 of them died from malaria, blasting accidents, or landslides. In winter, the Ottawa section becomes a natural skating rink.

⏱ 60 min
3

ByWard Market, operating since 1826, is one of Canada's oldest public markets. Its open-air stalls occupy several blocks around George Street and ByWard Street. Québec cheeses, maple syrup, Ottawa Valley farm products, Indigenous crafts. The surrounding neighborhood teems with restaurants and bars.

⏱ 75 min
4

The National Gallery of Canada occupies a granite-and-glass building designed by Moshe Safdie, inaugurated in 1988. Its glass-walled Great Hall frames Parliament Hill directly. It houses the country's most important Canadian and Indigenous art collections, plus a remarkable Inuit collection—soapstone carvings, prints, and textiles.

⏱ 90 min

Montreal

🛏️ Suggested base : Montreal
★★★★☆
1

Place d'Armes is Montréal's historic heart. At its center, the statue of Maisonneuve, the city's founder in 1642, faces Notre-Dame Basilica. Surrounding the square: the Old Seminary of Saint-Sulpice (1685), the oldest building still standing in Montréal, and the Bank of Montréal tower (1847), Canada's first bank.

⏱ 45 min
2

Montréal's Notre-Dame Basilica, built from 1824 to 1829 by Irish-born architect James O'Donnell, who was based in New York, is Canada's first Gothic Revival church. It could accommodate up to 10,000 worshippers—for 50 years it was the largest church in North America across all denominations. The interior, renovated from 1872 to 1880 by architect Victor Bourgeau, is a profusion of carved wood, gilding, and deep blue. The great bell “Jean-Baptiste”, installed in 1848, weighs 10,900 kg. O'Donnell, born Protestant, converted to Catholicism before his death in 1830; he rests in the crypt of the church he designed.

⏱ 75 min
3

The Old Port extends 2.5 kilometers along the St. Lawrence River. A former industrial port area that had fallen into disuse, it was redeveloped between 1983 and 1992 as a public promenade. Converted warehouses house the Montréal Science Centre. Across the water, on Île Sainte-Hélène, you can see the Biosphere—the former U.S. pavilion from Expo 67, a geodesic dome designed by Buckminster Fuller.

⏱ 60 min
4

Rue Saint-Paul, Montréal's oldest street, runs through Old Montréal between Place Royale and Bonsecours Market. The latter, built in 1847 in neoclassical style with its silver dome, served as a public market, city hall, and even the parliament of the Province of Canada. Cobblestones, cast-iron lampposts, and gray stone facades give the neighborhood a European atmosphere.

Montreal

🛏️ Suggested base : Montreal
★★★★☆
1

Mount Royal Park, designed by Frederick Law Olmsted (the landscape architect who also designed Central Park in New York), opened in 1876. Olmsted Road, the park's main trail, winds gently upward through 200 hectares of urban forest to the Kondiaronk Belvedere, named for the Wendat chief who signed the Great Peace of Montréal with the French in 1701. The view sweeps across downtown, the St. Lawrence River, and, on clear days, the Adirondack Mountains to the south. The Mount Royal Chalet, in Beaux-Arts style, displays paintings by Québec artists like Marc-Aurèle Fortin and Paul-Émile Borduas. The summit cross, standing 31 meters tall, was erected in 1924.

⏱ 120 min
2

The Plateau Mont-Royal is recognizable by its exterior spiral staircases, characteristic of Montréal housing built between 1880 and 1930—a solution to maximize interior space on narrow lots. Avenue Mont-Royal, the neighborhood's commercial thoroughfare, is lined with a mix of independent cafés, used bookstores, and terraces. The neighborhood was home to francophone artists and writers for decades.

⏱ 75 min
3

Mile End is Montréal's creative neighborhood, wedged between Boulevard Saint-Laurent and Avenue du Parc. Its former textile workshops have been converted into artist studios and technology companies. This is where Montréal's two rival bagel bakeries bake in wood-fired ovens day and night, as they have since the 1950s—bagels smaller, denser, and sweeter than New York's, hand-rolled and poached in honey water. The neighborhood's walls are covered with murals: Montréal has more than 300 works of street art.

⏱ 75 min

Activities

Wood-fired bagel tasting in Mile End, followed by coffee at one of the neighborhood's independent roasters.

⏱ 30 min

Mauricie

🛏️ Suggested base : Mauricie
★★★☆☆
1

La Mauricie National Park, created in 1970, protects 536 km² of Canadian Shield—rock formations among the continent's oldest, formed a billion years ago. Glaciers sculpted the rounded hills and carved over 150 lakes connected by portages. The Promenade Road crosses the park for 63 kilometers from entrance to entrance, through mixed forest of sugar maples, yellow birches, and balsam firs. Indigenous rock paintings were discovered on a cliff at Lac Wapizagonke, 17 kilometers long.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Lac-Solitaire trail hike: a gradual ascent through a mature maple forest, with lookouts offering views down over the surrounding lakes. At the summit, a solar-heated warming cabin provides a place to rest. The park is home to 50 mammal species (eastern wolf, black bear, moose, beaver) and 180 bird species, including the common loon, the park's emblem.

⏱ 180 min

Canoeing on one of the park's 150 lakes. Rentals available at Shewenegan, Wapizagonke, or Lac-Édouard picnic areas. Before 1970, this territory was exploited by logging companies and private hunting and fishing clubs reserved for an anglophone elite. The park ended these privileges to open nature to all.

⏱ 120 min
CarDrive to next stop: 157 km · 2h25

Quebec

🛏️ Suggested base : Quebec
★★★★★
1

Place Royale, in the Lower Town, marks the exact spot where Samuel de Champlain built his first habitation on July 3, 1608. Notre-Dame-des-Victoires church, erected in 1688, is the oldest stone church in Canada. The gabled houses bordering the square, with steep roofs and stone walls, evoke a Norman village. Champlain chose this site for the river's narrowing, which offered a natural defensive advantage—"Kebec" means "where the river narrows" in Algonquin.

⏱ 75 min
2

The Petit-Champlain quarter, at the cliff's base, is linked to the Upper Town by the funicular (inaugurated 1879) or the Breakneck Stairs. Its cobbled streets, among North America's oldest, shelter artisan boutiques, leather workshops, and cafés. In winter, decorations transform the quarter into a Christmas village.

⏱ 60 min
3

Château Frontenac, designed by American architect Bruce Price for the Canadian Pacific Railway Company, opened in 1893. Built on the very site of Champlain's fort and Château Saint-Louis, it was expanded several times: the Citadel wing in 1899, the Mont-Carmel wing in 1908, the central tower in 1924. In 1943, Churchill and Roosevelt met here to plan the Normandy landings. Dufferin Terrace, a long wooden boardwalk alongside it, overlooks the St. Lawrence River from 60 meters up.

⏱ 60 min
4

Québec's fortifications form 4.6 kilometers of ramparts with gates, cannons, and towers. Québec is the only walled city in North America north of Mexico whose walls remain intact. The first palisades date from Champlain in 1608. The current ramparts were built between 1693 and 1871, blending French (Vauban-style) and British influences. The Citadel, built between 1820 and 1850, is the largest British fortress in North America; it's still occupied by the Royal 22e Régiment, the Canadian Armed Forces' only francophone regiment.

⏱ 75 min

Quebec

🛏️ Suggested base : Quebec
★★★★★
1

Montmorency Falls plunges 83 meters into the St. Lawrence River—30 meters taller than Niagara Falls. Fed by the Montmorency River cascading down the Laurentian escarpment, it bears the name of the Duke of Montmorency, viceroy of New France in the 17th century. A suspension bridge at the summit crosses the curtain of mist. In winter, spray freezes at the base of the falls to form an ice cone called the “sugar loaf”, which Québécois have been climbing since the 19th century.

⏱ 90 min
2

Île d'Orléans, connected to the mainland by a single bridge since 1935, preserves rural Québec. Six parishes founded in the 17th century (the oldest, Sainte-Famille, dates from 1661) line a 67-kilometer road circling the island. Maple groves, apple orchards, strawberry fields, and vineyards form an agricultural landscape that has barely changed since French rule. The island has over 600 heritage buildings, some dating from the 18th century.

⏱ 120 min
3

Sainte-Anne-de-Beaupré Basilica, a pilgrimage destination since 1658, attracts hundreds of thousands of visitors each year. The current Romanesque Revival building dates from 1926—the two previous basilicas were destroyed by fires. Its facade is flanked by two 91-meter bell towers. Inside, 240 stained-glass windows tell the story of Saint Anne's life. The crutches and prostheses hung at the entrance bear witness to healings that pilgrims attribute to the saint. Allow for a round trip from Québec during the day to visit Sainte-Anne-de-Beaupré.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Tour of Île d'Orléans by car or bicycle, with stops at farms to taste ice cider, artisan cheeses, and homemade jams. Producers open their doors from May to October.

Baie Saint Paul

🛏️ Suggested base : Baie Saint Paul
★★★☆☆
1

From Baie-Saint-Paul, take Route 362 toward La Malbaie: it follows the St. Lawrence River through Les Éboulements and offers panoramic views of the mountains of Charlevoix. Stop at viewpoints overlooking the river before returning to Baie-Saint-Paul. Charlevoix has been a UNESCO World Biosphere Reserve since 1988 because of its astrobleme—a meteorite impact crater 56 km in diameter and 400 million years old that shaped the region's entire geography.

⏱ 60 min
2

Baie-Saint-Paul, nestled in a valley where the Gouffre River meets the St. Lawrence, is nicknamed the “Land of Artists”. Painters like Clarence Gagnon, Marc-Aurèle Fortin, and Jean-Paul Lemieux began setting up their easels here in the early 20th century. Rue Saint-Jean-Baptiste has Canada's highest density of art galleries. Cirque du Soleil was also born in Baie-Saint-Paul in 1984, emerging from a street performers' troupe led by a fire-breather from Québec City. The Museum of Contemporary Art has organized an international symposium every summer since 1982.

⏱ 90 min
3

Isle-aux-Coudres, accessible by free ferry from Saint-Joseph-de-la-Rive (15 minutes), is an 11-kilometer-long island in the river. Jacques Cartier named it in 1535 for the many hazel trees he found there. Cycling around the island covers roughly 23 kilometers on a flat road. You can visit two mills designated as historic monuments: a water mill and a windmill, both built in the 19th century, which operated in tandem until 1948.

⏱ 120 min

Activities

Bicycle tour of Isle-aux-Coudres: 23-kilometer loop on flat road, with stops at heritage mills, artisan shops, and river viewpoints. Bike rentals available near the dock.

⏱ 120 min

La Malbaie

🛏️ Suggested base : Tadoussac · 63 km
★★★☆☆
1

Parc national des Hautes-Gorges-de-la-Rivière-Malbaie protects deeply incised valleys whose cliffs rank among the highest east of the Canadian Rockies. The Malbaie River carved these spectacular gorges over millions of years. The name “Acropole des Draveurs” comes from a poem written by Félix-Antoine Savard in 1972, honoring the log drivers—workers who, each spring as the snow melted, threw felled tree trunks into the river to send them to mills downstream. Allow for a round trip from La Malbaie during the day to reach the park.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Acropole des Draveurs hike: 10.8 kilometers round-trip, 800 meters of elevation gain, summit at an elevation of 1,048 meters. The first two kilometers climb steeply on natural stone steps. Beyond, the maple forest gives way to alpine tundra. Three successive summits offer views down over the Malbaie River, 800 meters below, and over the Charlevoix mountain ranges. At the third summit, there is a view of two glacial lakes. Bring warm clothing: it's 5 to 10°C cooler at the summit, which is very exposed to the wind.

⏱ 360 min

Alternative for those who do not want to take the long hike: a rabaska (large canoe) or sightseeing boat cruise on the Malbaie River at the bottom of the gorges. Views looking up at the towering, forest-covered rock faces. The Le Riverain trail (easy, 4 km) follows the river with viewpoints overlooking the cliffs.

⏱ 120 min

Tadoussac

🛏️ Suggested base : Tadoussac
★★★★☆
1

Drive toward Tadoussac on Route 138 along the river. The road passes through Charlevoix villages (Saint-Siméon, Baie-des-Rochers), with glimpses of the St. Lawrence as it grows ever wider. At Baie-Sainte-Catherine, a free ferry crosses the Saguenay River in 10 minutes. From the ferry deck, you can see the mouth of the Saguenay Fjord, less than a kilometer wide here. With a little luck, you may already spot belugas from the boat.

⏱ 30 min
2

Tadoussac, a village of 800, occupies one of the world's most beautiful bays (member of the Club of the Most Beautiful Bays in the World since 1998). Jacques Cartier anchored here in 1535. In 1600, Pierre Chauvin founded New France's first fur trading post—eight years before Champlain founded Québec City. A reconstruction of the trading post, facing the beach, traces the history of exchanges with First Nations.

⏱ 60 min
3

The Chapelle des Indiens (1747) is Canada's oldest surviving wooden chapel, with its red roof and white walls. Hôtel Tadoussac, recognizable by its large red roof, has welcomed visitors since 1864—the first tourists arrived by steamboat from Montréal and Québec. The dunes of Tadoussac form a striking landscape of sand and forest along the river.

Tadoussac

🛏️ Suggested base : Tadoussac
★★★★☆
1

The Marine Mammal Interpretation Centre (CIMM), run by the Group for Research and Education on Marine Mammals (GREMM), presents the 13 cetacean species frequenting the St. Lawrence estuary. Life-size skeletons include a 13-meter sperm whale. Centre naturalists individually track humpback whales and belugas, identifying them by skin or fluke markings.

⏱ 60 min
2

Parc national du Fjord-du-Saguenay protects the shores of the glacial fjord. Carved by glaciers 10,000 years ago, the Saguenay reaches a depth of 276 meters at Cap Éternité, but only 20 meters at its mouth—glacial debris formed a natural sill. At Baie Éternité, the cliffs of Cap Trinité rise more than 300 meters straight above the fjord's black water. The Sentier de la Statue (3.5 km, 1 hour 30 minutes) leads to a 9-meter statue of the Virgin Mary installed at the top of the cliff in 1881. Visiting Baie Éternité requires a round trip during the day from Tadoussac, crossing the Saguenay by ferry and then continuing by road.

⏱ 120 min

Activities

Whale-watching cruise in the St. Lawrence estuary at the Saguenay confluence. The meeting of the Saguenay's cold, fresh water with the river's saltwater creates upwellings that bring krill and small fish to the surface, attracting cetaceans. From May to October, you can observe minke whales, fin whales (second-largest animal on Earth), humpback whales, and occasionally blue whales. St. Lawrence belugas, a resident population of roughly 1,700, frequent the area year-round.
