New Delhi

🛏️ Suggested base : New Delhi
★★★★★
1

Start with the Red Fort (Lal Qila), commissioned by Shah Jahan in 1639 and completed in nine years. Its red sandstone walls soar up to 33 meters on the city side—you'll spot them from afar from Chandni Chowk. Inside, don't miss the Diwan-i-Khas (Hall of Private Audiences), which once housed the famous Peacock Throne, stolen by the Persians in 1739. The Nahr-i-Behisht, a canal that flowed through the pavilions, evoked the Quranic paradise. Allow 1 hour 30 minutes to tour the 120-acre complex, a UNESCO site since 2007.

⏱ 90 min
2

A five-minute walk from the Red Fort, Jama Masjid awaits you. Shah Jahan had it built between 1650 and 1656 by 5,000 craftsmen, for one million rupees. Its inner courtyard can accommodate 25,000 worshippers. If you feel up to it, climb the 130 steps of the southern minaret - the panoramic view takes in all of Old Delhi as far as Connaught Place, an alignment intended by the British architect Lutyens. Remove your shoes and cover your shoulders before entering.

⏱ 60 min
3

Push on to Chandni Chowk, Delhi's oldest market, designed in 1650 by Princess Jahanara, Shah Jahan's favorite daughter. The name means 'moonlight square' - a central pool once reflected the moon. Today the canal is gone but the chaos remains. Lose yourself in Khari Baoli, Asia's largest spice market, where mountains of turmeric and chili make your eyes sting. Dariba Kalan has been the jewelers' street since Mughal times. Parathe Wali Gali has served stuffed parathas for generations - it's the obligatory street food stop.

⏱ 90 min
4

Walking up Chandni Chowk, stop at Gurudwara Sis Ganj Sahib, built at the spot where the ninth Sikh guru, Tegh Bahadur, was executed by the Mughals in 1675 for refusing to convert to Islam. The temple welcomes everyone - you can watch devotees at prayer and, if you wish, join the langar (free communal meal). Cover your head with a scarf provided at the entrance.

⏱ 40 min
5

End the day at Raj Ghat, Gandhi's memorial, on the banks of the Yamuna. A simple black marble platform marks the spot where the Mahatma was cremated on January 31, 1948, one day after his assassination. An eternal flame burns constantly. The place is stark and moving - plan to remove your shoes. The surrounding park is planted with trees gifted by world leaders.

⏱ 40 min

New Delhi

🛏️ Suggested base : New Delhi
★★★★★
1

New Delhi brings a change of atmosphere. Begin with Humayun's Tomb, the Indian subcontinent's first garden-tomb and the Taj Mahal's prototype. Built in 1570 by Empress Bega Begum for her husband, the second Mughal emperor, it blends red sandstone and white marble in a Persian garden divided into four sections (charbagh). The central dome rises to 47 meters. The interior chambers hold the tombs of more than 150 Mughal family members—hence the nickname “dormitory of the Mughals.” A UNESCO site since 1993, it has been beautifully restored by the Aga Khan Trust.

⏱ 90 min
2

Head to Qutub Minar, 72.5 meters tall. Construction began in 1199 under Qutb-ud-din Aibak, after his victory over the last Hindu king of Delhi, and was completed by his successor Iltutmish around 1220. The tower has five stories, 379 steps (closed to the public since a fatal accident in 1981 in which 45 people died in a stampede), and narrows from 14.3 m in diameter at the base to 2.7 m at the top. Do not miss the 4th-century iron pillar in the courtyard—it has resisted rust for 1,600 years and no one really knows why.

⏱ 75 min
3

Pass by India Gate, Delhi's triumphal arch, 42 meters tall, built in 1931 to honor the 70,000 Indian soldiers who died in World War I. Since 2022, an eternal flame has burned beneath the nearby canopy, the National War Memorial. In the evening, the monument is illuminated and the esplanade becomes a place for family strolls—join the Delhiites picnicking on the lawn.

⏱ 40 min
4

If you still have energy, detour to the Lotus Temple, a Bahá'í House of Worship completed in 1986, whose lotus-shaped structure has 27 white marble petals. Iranian-Canadian architect Fariborz Sahba drew inspiration from the lotus, a symbol common to Hinduism, Buddhism, and Islam. Inside is a vast silent meditation space—no sermons, no religious images. Enter, sit down, and enjoy the calm after Delhi's chaos.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Stroll around Lodhi Gardens, a 36-hectare park housing tombs from the Sayyid and Lodi dynasties (15th–16th centuries) amid manicured lawns. Delhiites jog here in the morning—it's the perfect spot to catch your breath between visits.

⏱ 45 min

Explore Connaught Place, the commercial heart of New Delhi, a set of white colonnaded buildings arranged in concentric circles, designed by the British in the 1930s. The restaurants and cafés lining the arcades are perfect for a lunch break. Head down to the underground Palika Bazaar if you fancy Indian-style shopping.

⏱ 60 min

Late in the day, visit the Nizamuddin Dargah, Delhi's most revered Sufi shrine, dating from the 14th century. On Thursday evenings, qawwali musicians sing mystical poetry in the courtyard - the atmosphere is intense and hypnotic. Cover your head as a sign of respect.

⏱ 60 min

Amritsar

🛏️ Suggested base : Amritsar
★★★★★
1

You arrive in Amritsar by train (allow around 6 hours from Delhi on the Shatabdi Express). Drop your bags and head straight for the Golden Temple—Harmandir Sahib. It's Sikhism's holiest site, and probably one of the world's most beautiful religious buildings. The temple sits at the center of a sacred pool measuring 150 m by 150 m (the Amrit Sarovar, “pool of nectar”), connected to land by a 60-meter marble causeway. Maharaja Ranjit Singh covered the upper stories with 750 kg of pure gold in the 1830s. Four entrances symbolize openness to all castes and religions. Arrive early in the morning to watch sunrise on the golden dome—the reflection in the water is breathtaking.

⏱ 120 min
2

Right next to the Golden Temple, push through the narrow gate of Jallianwala Bagh. This is where on April 13, 1919, British General Dyer ordered his troops to fire on an unarmed crowd of thousands gathered for the Baisakhi festival. About 379 dead officially - likely far more. Bullet marks are still visible on the walls, and the well into which dozens jumped to escape the gunfire is preserved. The Flame of Liberty memorial, a 9-meter red sandstone pylon, was inaugurated in 1961. The place is stark and poignant - take time to read the panels.

⏱ 60 min
3

Don't miss the Partition Museum, housed in Amritsar's old town hall. It traces the 1947 division of India and Pakistan through testimonies, personal objects, and photographs. It's one of the few museums worldwide dedicated to this event, which displaced 15 million people and killed between one and two million.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

In the late afternoon, drive to the Wagah border (28 km, 45 min). Every evening at sunset, Indian and Pakistani border guards perform the “Beating Retreat” ceremony—a surreal spectacle of high kicks, exaggerated martial postures, and glares, followed by the simultaneous lowering of both flags. 25,000 spectators on the Indian side, a soccer stadium atmosphere. Arrive at least 1 hour 30 minutes before the start to get a good seat.

⏱ 150 min

Eat at the Golden Temple langar - the world's largest free kitchen. Every day over 50,000 meals are prepared and served by volunteers: dal, chapati, vegetables, and kheer. Everyone is welcome, without distinction. Sit in rows on the floor and let yourself be served. The experience is as spiritual as it is gastronomic.

⏱ 45 min

If you return from Wagah in the evening, go back to the Golden Temple at night. The illuminated building is reflected in the pool—it's even more magical than by day. Chants from the Guru Granth Sahib echo in the cool air and devotees circle the pool. A moment of absolute peace after the tumult of the Wagah ceremony.

Dharamshala

🛏️ Suggested base : Dharamshala
★★★★☆
1

Dharamsala is about 5 hours by road from Amritsar—the road gradually climbs toward the Himalayan foothills. Your destination is McLeod Ganj, “little Lhasa,” perched at an altitude of 1,770 m. The Dalai Lama has lived here since 1959, after fleeing Chinese-occupied Tibet. Begin with the Tsuglagkhang complex, the spiritual heart of the Tibetan community in exile. The main temple houses a 3-meter golden statue of Buddha Shakyamuni, flanked by Avalokiteshvara and Padmasambhava. The atmosphere is peaceful—monks in crimson robes, prayer wheels spinning, whispers of mantras.

⏱ 90 min
2

Right next door, the Kalachakra Temple contains frescoes depicting the 722 deities of the mandala, painted over three years by three master painters under the Dalai Lama's personal direction. Photography is forbidden, but the level of detail in the mandalas is stunning.

⏱ 30 min
3

Visit the Tibet Museum at Gangchen Kyishong in Dharamsala, within the Central Tibetan Administration complex. It traces the history of Tibet as an independent nation, Chinese occupation since 1949, and exile. The testimonies of political prisoners and archival photographs are deeply moving. Allow at least 45 minutes.

⏱ 45 min
4

Walk down to Namgyal Monastery, the Dalai Lama's personal monastery, founded in 1564. If you're there around 1:30pm (except Sundays), you can watch the monks' debates in the courtyard - a fascinating spectacle where arguments are punctuated with theatrical hand-clapping. The monks are usually delighted to chat with visitors.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Walk to Bhagsu Waterfall, about 2 km from McLeod Ganj. The walk passes through Bhagsu village and climbs among rocks toward the waterfall. Bring walking shoes. At the top, several cafés with valley views serve momos (Tibetan dumplings) and butter tea.

⏱ 90 min

Wander McLeod Ganj's lanes - Tibetan shops sell singing bowls, thangkas (fabric paintings), woolen shawls, and turquoise jewelry. Cafés serve authentic Tibetan cuisine: thukpa (noodle soup), steamed or fried momos, and the famous yak butter tea. A world apart from the Indian plains.

⏱ 60 min

Take a thangka painting or Tibetan cooking class—several centers in McLeod Ganj offer half-day workshops. It's a hands-on way to connect with Tibetan culture beyond superficial tourism.

⏱ 120 min

Manali

🛏️ Suggested base : Manali
★★★★☆
1

The Dharamsala to Manali road is long (about 8 hours) but spectacular—it winds through the Kangra and Kullu valleys, between apple orchards, turquoise rivers, and snowy peaks. Plan stops for the views. Once in Manali, settle in and start with Hadimba Devi Temple, nestled in a cedar forest 2 km from the town center. Built in 1553 by Maharaja Bahadur Singh, it's a four-tiered wooden and stone pagoda temple, dedicated to Hadimba, a demoness from the Mahabharata who became a goddess after marrying the warrior Bhima. The architecture is unique in India—the wooden doors are adorned with detailed carvings of mythological figures.

⏱ 60 min
2

Climb up to Old Manali, 2 km upstream from the modern town. This stone and wood village has kept its Himalayan charm despite the influx of trendy cafés and guesthouses. Traditional wooden and slate houses cling to the slope, and lanes open onto unobstructed views toward the peaks. This is where the hippie community settled in the 1970s—the vibe remains laid-back.

⏱ 60 min
3

Detour to Vashisht, a hot-spring village 3 km from Manali. Natural hot springs feed public pools where locals and travelers bathe side by side. The Vashisht temple, dedicated to the Vedic sage of the same name, is a fine example of Himachali wooden architecture with detailed carvings on the lintels.

⏱ 45 min

Manali

🛏️ Suggested base : Manali
★★★★☆
1

Your second day in Manali is devoted to the higher elevations. Head to Solang Valley (14 km, 30 min), a natural amphitheater surrounded by snowy peaks. Depending on the season, you'll find paragliding, zorbing, and quad rides (summer), or skiing and sledding (winter). The gondola takes you up to an altitude of 3,000 m with views down into the Kullu Valley. Arrive early—crowds show up after 10 am.

⏱ 120 min
2

Take the road from Manali to the village of Kothi and allow for a round trip during the day. On the road to Rohtang, this village overlooks a deep gorge carved by the Beas. Stop at the viewpoint beside the road to see the river confined between rocky walls, then explore the village on foot. The houses and wooded slopes form a foreground to the snow-covered peaks. This stop lets you see the valley scenery without going all the way up to the pass. Bring warm clothes for outdoor stops.

⏱ 180 min
3

If Rohtang is closed, take the Atal Tunnel instead, the longest high-altitude road tunnel in the world (9.02 km), inaugurated in 2020. It connects the Kullu Valley to Lahaul in 30 minutes instead of 4 to 6 hours via the pass. On the other side, the village of Sissu and its waterfall offer a first glimpse of the desert landscapes of Lahaul - a striking contrast with the greenery of Manali. Allow for a round trip during the day from Manali through the Atal Tunnel to visit Sissu.

⏱ 120 min

Activities

Try tandem paragliding from Solang Valley or Marhi—Himalayan updrafts carry you above the valley for 15 to 20 minutes. The sensation of soaring with the 6,000-meter mountain range before you is unforgettable. Operators offer the flight with GoPro video included.

⏱ 60 min

Stop at Vashisht hot springs on the way back if you didn't visit them yesterday. After a day at altitude, a bath in naturally hot sulfurous water is the best remedy for fatigue. Locals come at dawn—late in the day you'll have more space.

Chandigarh

🛏️ Suggested base : Chandigarh
★★★☆☆
1

Chandigarh is a transit stop between Manali and Rishikesh (307 km from Manali, about 6 hours 35 minutes by road). But the city is worth a stop: it's the only Indian city planned by Le Corbusier in the 1950s, after Partition. The Capitol Complex, UNESCO-listed in 2016, brings together the High Court, Secretariat, and Assembly—a monumental complex of raw concrete that is surprising in the Indian context. The Open Hand, a 26-meter-tall sculpture, turns freely with the wind and symbolizes peace.

⏱ 75 min
2

Do not miss the Rock Garden, the work of one man - Nek Chand, a roads inspector who secretly built a sculpture garden from recycled urban waste (pottery fragments, construction rubble, broken bangles) on 16 hectares of government land. Discovered in 1975, the site faced demolition, but public support saved it. The mosaic figures lined up by the hundreds create a striking visual effect.

⏱ 75 min
3

If you have an hour free, walk along Sukhna Lake, an artificial lake created in 1958 by building a dam across a seasonal stream. Locals take their evening stroll here, and boats are available. The late-day light on the water with the Shivaliks in the background is superb.

Rishikesh

🛏️ Suggested base : Rishikesh
★★★☆☆
1

Rishikesh - the 'world capital of yoga' - sits where the Ganges leaves the Himalayan foothills for the plains. The atmosphere is radically different from Delhi's chaos: here cows, sadhus in orange, and backpackers in yoga pants coexist on the ghats. Start by crossing Ram Jhula, the 137-meter suspension bridge built in 1986, linking ashrams on both banks. The view of the turquoise Ganges, framed by forested hills, sets the tone.

⏱ 30 min
2

Visit the Beatles Ashram (officially Chaurasi Kutia), Maharishi Mahesh Yogi's former ashram where John Lennon, Paul McCartney, George Harrison, and Ringo Starr stayed in 1968 to study Transcendental Meditation. Abandoned for decades, the site has reopened to visitors and the ruined buildings are covered with colorful graffiti and murals honoring the Fab Four. They composed much of the White Album here. The place is strangely peaceful despite its fame.

⏱ 60 min
3

Walk down to Swarg Ashram, the pedestrian quarter on the Ganges' left bank. No cars here—only lanes lined with ashrams, temples, and small vegetarian restaurants. Bharat Mandir, Rishikesh's oldest temple, is dedicated to Vishnu and is believed to date from the 12th century.

⏱ 45 min
4

Late in the day, head to Triveni Ghat or Parmarth Niketan for Ganga Aarti—the evening ceremony where dozens of priests wave large oil lamps while facing the river as mantras resound. Hundreds of small floating lamps (diyas) are placed on the water. The atmosphere is intense and deeply moving—arrive 30 minutes early to find a good spot on the steps.

🛏️ Suggested base : Khajuraho
★★★★☆
1

Khajuraho is about 4 hours by road from Orchha. UNESCO-listed since 1986, it's one of India's most extraordinary sites. Of the 85 temples built by the Chandela dynasty between 950 and 1050 AD, 25 have survived—and their sculptures are stunningly refined. Start with the Western Group, the most spectacular. 2

Take time to walk all the way around the temple to appreciate the progression of the sculptures, from the earthly world at the base toward the divine at the top.

⏱ 45 min
3

Don't neglect the Eastern Group, 20 minutes' walk away. The Jain temples of Parsvanath and Adinath are less famous but equally finely carved, with floral motifs and apsaras of remarkable grace. The contrast between Hindu and Jain temples side by side testifies to the Chandela era's religious tolerance.

Train from Kolkata to Bhubaneswar (about 7 hours on the Shatabdi). The 'city of a thousand temples' closes this circuit with a return to Indian spirituality's roots. Lingaraj Temple, Bhubaneswar's largest and most venerated, dates from the 11th century. Its 55-meter tower (deul) dominates the city - it's the finest example of Kalinga architectural style. Entry is reserved for Hindus, but you can observe the temple from a raised platform outside the enclosure, originally installed for Lord Curzon.

⏱ 45 min
2

Rajarani Temple (11th century) is nicknamed the “love temple” for its sculptures of entwined couples and celestial nymphs recalling Khajuraho, on a smaller scale. Unlike Lingaraj, it's open to all. The surrounding garden is peaceful—the sandstone sculptures, turned golden by the late afternoon light, are magnificent.

⏱ 40 min
3

Visit Udayagiri and Khandagiri caves, carved in the 2nd century BC by King Kharavela to house Jain monks. Udayagiri ('sunrise hill') has 18 caves adorned with detailed bas-reliefs showing court scenes and elephant processions. Rani Gumpha ('queen's cave'), on two levels, is the most spectacular. Khandagiri, opposite, offers a panoramic view over Bhubaneswar from its summit.

⏱ 75 min
4

If you have time, detour to Dhauli (8 km south), where Emperor Ashoka, after the bloody Battle of Kalinga in 261 BC that killed over 100,000, had a crisis of conscience that converted him to Buddhism. The rock edicts of Ashoka carved in stone are among India's oldest texts. The white Shanti Stupa (Peace Stupa), built in 1972 by the Japanese at the hilltop, offers unobstructed views over rice paddies.

⏱ 60 min
