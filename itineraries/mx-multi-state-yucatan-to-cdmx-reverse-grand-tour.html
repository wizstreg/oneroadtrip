1

The site of Cobá, reached by car from Tulum, covers 80 km² in the jungle, with its structures connected by 50 white Mayan roads (sacbeob). Rent a bicycle at the entrance: it is 4 km to Nohoch Mul, the main pyramid, which is 42 meters high. It reopened for climbing in December 2025 with a new wooden stairway. The summit offers a 360° view over the canopy.

⏱ 150 min
2

Cenote Dos Ojos, 20 km from Tulum, is actually two cenotes linked by a 60-meter underground tunnel. The system forms part of Sac Actun—350 km mapped. The water stays at 25–26°C all year round.

⏱ 90 min

Activities

Snorkeling or diving at Cenote Dos Ojos—the Barbie Line route (515 meters, 6 meters deep) winds between stalactites and fossils. You'll need two flashlights. On-site operators provide equipment and guides for both circuits.

⏱ 90 min

Bike ride along Cobá's trails between structures buried in vegetation. You might spot spider monkeys or coatis. Only 10% of the site has been excavated—the rest sleeps beneath the jungle.

🛏️ Suggested base : Merida
★★★★☆
1

Chichén Itzá early in the morning—tour buses arrive after 10am, so be there at 8am. The Pyramid of Kukulcán stands 30 meters tall with four staircases of 91 steps each: 364 total, plus the temple platform, equals 365—one for each day of the year. During the spring and autumn equinoxes, the shadow of the corners draws the undulating body of a serpent descending toward the carved head at the base of the stairs.

The Franciscan Convent of San Antonio de Padua (1561) was built on the ruins of the Mayan pyramid Pap-Hol-Chac—its 7,806-square-meter atrium is the second largest in the world after St. Peter's Square. Pope John Paul II celebrated mass here in 1993.

🛏️ Suggested base : Merida

1

Uxmal, 80 kilometers from Mérida, is one of the most beautiful sites in Yucatán and far less crowded than Chichén Itzá. The Pyramid of the Magician (28 meters) is the only Mayan pyramid with an elliptical floor plan. Its facade is entirely covered with masks of Chaac, the rain god—hundreds of hooked noses stacked for several meters. The Nunnery Quadrangle below is a masterpiece of geometric mosaic.

⏱ 150 min
2

Kabáh, 20 kilometers south of Uxmal on the Puuc route, features the Codz Poop: an entire facade covered with 250 Chaac masks, overlapping from ground to summit. It's one of the most densely decorated facades in all Mayan architecture.

⏱ 75 min

Activities

The Uxmal sound and light show happens every evening at dusk—colorful projections illuminate the temples and Chaac masks. A spectacle that transforms the site. Ask about showtimes at the entrance.

⏱ 60 min

Puuc route by car: between Uxmal and Kabáh, several smaller sites follow in sequence—Sayil, Labná (with its Mayan triumphal arch), Xlapak. Few crowds, plenty of character. Worth doing at your own pace if you have a car.

Palenque

🛏️ Suggested base : Palenque
★★★★★
1

The Temple of the Inscriptions is Palenque's main pyramid—22 meters high, with 617 Mayan glyphs on its walls. In 1952, archaeologist Alberto Ruz Lhuillier discovered a secret stairway descending into the heart of the pyramid: the tomb of King Pakal, who died in 683 after a 68-year reign. His 13-tonne sarcophagus is still there.

⏱ 120 min
2

The Royal Palace is a labyrinthine complex of courtyards, corridors, and chambers stacked on a 100-by-80-meter platform. Its four-story tower served as an astronomical observatory—the only tower of its kind in Mayan architecture. The wall reliefs recount military victories and court rituals under Pakal's reign.

⏱ 90 min

Activities

Arrive when the site opens (8am). Howler monkeys start calling at dawn—a deep, primal sound rising from the jungle below the temples. It's one of Mexico's most powerful auditory memories.

⏱ 30 min

Alberto Ruz Lhuillier Museum, included with site admission: 200 pieces recovered from Palenque, including a scale reproduction of Pakal's tomb with its famous jade mask. Don't miss the Otolum waterfalls in the park before heading to the museum—cold, clear water.

★★★★☆
1

San Juan Chamula, 10 kilometers from San Cristóbal, is a Tzotzil village entirely autonomous from the Mexican government—it has its own laws and police. The Church of San Juan Bautista is Chiapas' most fascinating place: no pews, the floor covered with pine needles and hundreds of candles. Shamans perform healing rituals with eggs, soda, and herbs. Photos strictly forbidden inside—and the ban is serious.

⏱ 90 min
2

The amber market on Real de Guadalupe is one of Mexico's best-stocked: Chiapas is one of the world's major amber producers. Pieces containing fossilized insects or plants are the rarest. Learn to distinguish real amber (it floats in salt water) from plastic imitations.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

San Cristóbal's craft market around Templo de Santo Domingo—Tzotzil textiles, Lacandon pottery, painted wooden figurines from Zinacantán. Women in black wool dresses with colored threads sell their wares directly on the forecourt.

⏱ 75 min

Tasting pox (pronounced 'posh')—the ceremonial corn liquor of the Tzotzils, not to be confused with mezcal. Several bars on Real de Guadalupe offer pox-based cocktails. An unusual local experience.

Sumidero Canyon: 30 km of vertical cliffs reaching up to 1,000 meters high, carved by the Grijalva River. Drive from San Cristóbal to Chiapa de Corzo to board the boat. Activities

Mezcal bar on Real de Guadalupe for your last evening in San Cristóbal. Chiapas mezcals (made from wild agave) are less famous than Oaxaca's but often wilder. Bartenders at the good establishments explain the differences between espadín, tobalá, and cuishe.

The Basilica of Our Lady of Solitude (1682) is Oaxaca's most revered sanctuary—the Virgin of Solitude wears a crown studded with 600 diamonds. The Baroque facade with its three porticoes is one of colonial Mexico's most elaborate. The interior is dark, fragrant with incense and fresh flowers brought by worshippers.

⏱ 45 min
2

The Templo de Santo Domingo (16th–17th century) has one of Mexico's most spectacular Baroque interiors: a vault covered in gilded stucco reliefs depicting the genealogical tree of the Guzmán family. Free admission. Next door, the Museum of Oaxacan Cultures houses the reconstruction of Monte Albán's Tomb 7 with its 500 gold and jade objects.

Activities

Benito Juárez market for lunch: Oaxaca in miniature under one roof. Chapulines (grasshoppers toasted with lemon), quesillo (string cheese), tasajo, and memelas. Try the chapulines if you get the chance—crunchy and mildly spicy.

Oaxaca street art: from the Zapatistas to contemporary artists, city walls tell Mexico's political history. The major murals are concentrated along the stretch between the Zócalo and Mercado de la Merced. Several galleries alternate between Zapotec indigenous art and contemporary Mexican art.

🛏️ Suggested base : Oaxaca De Juarez
★★★★★
1

Monte Albán, 10 kilometers from Oaxaca, is the quintessential Zapotec capital—founded around 500 BCE, it reached 35,000 inhabitants at its peak. The site sits on an artificially leveled mountain at 1,941 meters elevation, 400 meters above the valley. The Gran Plaza (300 by 150 meters) is surrounded by temples, a ball court, and an astronomical observatory. 360-degree panoramic views over Oaxaca's three valleys.

⏱ 150 min
2

San Bartolo Coyotepec, 12 kilometers from Oaxaca, is the village of black pottery (barro negro)—a Zapotec technique several centuries old. Pieces, entirely hand-modeled and fired in underground kilns, take on their black color from contact with smoke. Some family workshops welcome visitors to see the process live.

⏱ 75 min

Activities

Arrive at Monte Albán at opening (8am) for morning light on the temples and relative coolness. By early afternoon, the heat and glare off the white stone are punishing. Plan 2 to 2.5 hours for a complete visit.

⏱ 30 min

Weaving workshop in Teotitlán del Valle—the village of Zapotec tapestries, 30 kilometers from Oaxaca. Rugs are woven on traditional looms with wool dyed with cochineal, indigo, and turmeric. Patterns reproduce Monte Albán glyphs.

Hierve el Agua, 70 kilometers from Oaxaca in the mountains, is a unique geological formation: springs supersaturated with calcium carbonate have created mineral waterfalls frozen on the cliff face—the same phenomenon as Pamukkale in Turkey, but Mexican-style. The large cascade is 60 meters high. Natural pools at the cliff's edge let you swim with views of the valley 200 meters below.

⏱ 120 min
2

Santiago Matatlan, reached by car from Oaxaca, describes itself as the “world capital of mezcal”—and that is no exaggeration. More than half the mezcals sold in Mexico come from this village. Operators offer visits to artisanal palenques (traditional distilleries), where you can see the earth oven used to cook agave piñas and the copper still.

⏱ 90 min

Activities

Swimming in the Hierve el Agua pools—the water is cool (22–25°C) and slightly mineralized. A 20-minute trail descends to view the petrified waterfalls from below. No barriers at the edge of the formations—be careful.

⏱ 75 min

Tasting and purchasing artisanal mezcal in Matatlan. At good palenques, they explain the difference between earth-oven cooking (smoky mezcal) and aboveground-oven cooking (milder mezcal), and between the different agaves used. Bring home a bottle—Matatlan mezcal is hard to find outside the region.

The Capilla del Rosario in the Church of Santo Domingo is nicknamed 'the eighth wonder of the world'—a side chapel entirely covered in gold leaf, sculpted stucco, and talavera tile from floor to ceiling. Built between 1650 and 1690, it's the finest example of churrigueresque baroque in Mexico. Free admission, and the effect is immediate.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Biblioteca Palafoxiana is the oldest public library in the Americas (1646), inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World register. Its 17th-century cedar shelves still hold 45,000 volumes including 15th-century incunabula. A visit takes less than an hour.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Mole tasting in a covered market—Mercado de la Victoria or El Parián market have comedores (small family eateries) where mole negro has been simmering since morning. Eat at the counter with locals.

Ciudad De Mexico

🛏️ Suggested base : Ciudad De Mexico
★★★★★
1

Mexico City's Zócalo is one of the world's largest squares—57,000 square meters, on the site of Tenochtitlan, the ancient Aztec capital. The National Palace on the east side occupies the site of Moctezuma's palace. Inside, Diego Rivera's murals (1929–1935) trace 3,000 years of Mexican history across 1,200 square meters of walls. Free admission.

⏱ 90 min
2

The Templo Mayor (excavated since 1978) is Tenochtitlan's main pyramid, uncovered directly beneath the historic center. The adjoining museum displays statuettes and objects from the eagle's tomb. Allow at least an hour.

⏱ 75 min

Activities

Coyoacán in the afternoon—Mexico City's most charming neighborhood. Frida Kahlo's House (Casa Azul) is painted cobalt and turquoise blue and showcases her works, studio, and life. Online reservation required. The Coyoacán craft market across the street sells reproductions of pre-Hispanic ceramics.

⏱ 120 min

Xochimilco as an alternative: plan a round trip during the day from central Mexico City. The trajineras (flower-decorated boats propelled with poles) travel through a network of preserved Aztec canals, one of the few remnants of the ancient lake city. On weekends, Mexican families fill the canals with their own boats and music. A floating celebration.
