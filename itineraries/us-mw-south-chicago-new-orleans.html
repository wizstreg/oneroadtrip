Chicago, IL

🛏️ Suggested base : Chicago, IL
★★★★★
1

Ascend to the Willis Tower Skydeck on the 103rd floor, 412 meters above the ground. When the building opened in 1973, it was the world's tallest tower - a title it held for 25 years. Today, it remains the highest observation deck in the United States. Dare to step onto The Ledge: glass boxes that extend 1.3 meters out into thin air. On clear days, the view spans four states. Pro tip: arrive 30 minutes before sunset to experience the city in daylight, twilight, and darkness.

⏱ 75 min
2

The Art Institute of Chicago ranks among the nation's greatest art museums, housing nearly 300,000 works. Installed since 1893 in a Beaux-Arts building constructed for the World's Fair, it holds the largest Impressionist and Post-Impressionist collection outside Europe - works by Monet, Renoir, Seurat. You'll also see Hopper's Nighthawks, Grant Wood's American Gothic, and Hokusai's The Great Wave. The Modern Wing, designed by Renzo Piano in 2009, adds 24,000 square meters. Budget at least 2 hours, though you could easily spend the day.

⏱ 150 min

Activities

Built in 1927 and modeled after Versailles's Latona Fountain, this 40-meter-diameter fountain stages water-and-light shows on summer evenings (May through mid-October). The setting with Chicago's skyline as backdrop is one of the city's most photographed scenes.

🛏️ Suggested base : Nashville, TN
★★★★☆
1

The Country Music Hall of Fame traces country music's evolution from Appalachian roots to today. The permanent collection includes instruments, stage costumes, and manuscripts from legends like Hank Williams, Patsy Cline, and Johnny Cash. The building itself, with its piano-key facade and tower evoking a radio antenna, nods to WSM, the station broadcasting the Grand Ole Opry since 1925.

⏱ 90 min

Step inside the Ryman Auditorium, dubbed the “Mother Church of Country Music.” Built in 1892 by Captain Thomas Ryman as an evangelical tabernacle, it hosted the Grand Ole Opry from 1943 to 1974. This is where Hank Williams was called back for six encores of “Lovesick Blues” in 1949. A 1.8-meter circle of wood was cut from the original stage and inlaid into the current Opry House stage. Take the backstage tour to see dressing rooms dedicated to individual artists - the hall's acoustics, likened to an old fiddle, are exceptional.

⏱ 60 min

Memphis, TN

🛏️ Suggested base : Memphis, TN
★★★★☆
1

The Stax Museum of American Soul Music occupies the exact site of the old Stax Records studio. From the 1960s, this studio shaped Memphis's soul sound - grittier and more brass-driven than Detroit's Motown. Otis Redding, Isaac Hayes, and Booker T. & the M.G.'s cut their hits here. The museum traces this history from Southern Baptist churches to hits that crossed the Atlantic. Don't miss the full-scale church recreation with its sound system - the chills are guaranteed.

⏱ 90 min
2

Sun Studio, at 706 Union Avenue, bills itself as the "birthplace of rock 'n' roll." In 1951, Sam Phillips recorded Jackie Brenston's "Rocket 88," considered the first rock record. In 1953, an 18-year-old Elvis Presley walked through the door to cut two songs for $3.25. The 45-minute guided tour puts you face-to-face with the original microphone - you can touch it. The studio still operates: after tours wrap, artists record through the night.

⏱ 60 min
3

The National Civil Rights Museum is built around the Lorraine Motel, where Martin Luther King Jr. was assassinated on April 4, 1968. Room 306, with its balcony, is preserved exactly as it was that day. The museum traces the complete history of the civil rights movement, from resistance to slavery through contemporary struggles. Budget at least 2 hours - it's an emotionally intense but essential visit.

⏱ 120 min

Activities

★★☆☆☆
1

On leaving Jackson, stop at Vicksburg National Military Park, about a 1 hour 30 minute drive from Natchez.
