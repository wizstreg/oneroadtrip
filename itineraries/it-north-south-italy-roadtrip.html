Verona

🛏️ Suggested base : Verona
★★★★★
1

The Arena of Verona, built around 30 AD in local pink stone, measures 138 meters long by 109 meters wide. Italy's third-largest Roman amphitheater after the Colosseum and Capua's arena, it once held approximately 30,000 spectators. An earthquake in 1117 destroyed nearly all of the outer ring; only a four-arch fragment called the Ala survives. Since 1913, the arena has hosted an open-air opera festival every summer: Aida, Carmen, and La Traviata performed before 22,000 spectators, each holding a candle.

⏱ 75 min
2

Piazza delle Erbe occupies the site of Verona's ancient Roman forum. Markets, medieval fountains, and Renaissance palaces cluster around this rectangle surrounded by frescoed facades. The column topped by the Lion of Saint Mark recalls Venetian rule over the city from 1405 onward.

⏱ 60 min
3

Casa di Giulietta, at Via Cappello 23, is a 14th-century medieval house traditionally linked to the Cappelletti family, who may have inspired Shakespeare's Capulets. The balcony was added in 1936 by the city to match the literary imagination. Millions of visitors stick love notes on the walls each year.

Bologna

🛏️ Suggested base : Bologna
★★★★★
1

Modena is approximately 46 km from Bologna, a 50-minute drive. Modena's Piazza Grande, listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1997 together with the cathedral and the Ghirlandina Tower, is a major medieval square in Italy. The Duomo di Modena, a Romanesque masterpiece begun in 1099 by the architect Lanfranco and the sculptor Wiligelmo, was consecrated by Pope Lucius in 1184. Its Porta della Pescheria, on the north side, is decorated with bas-reliefs depicting the twelve months and scenes from the legend of King Arthur - a rare combination in Italian Romanesque art.

Bologna

🛏️ Suggested base : Bologna
★★★★★
1

Piazza Maggiore has been Bologna's gravitational center since the Middle Ages. The Basilica of San Petronio, begun in 1390 and still unfinished, was originally intended to surpass St. Peter's in Rome—the Pope opposed this, diverting funds to a nearby college. Its facade, half-clad in marble and half in bare brick, testifies to this forced halt to construction.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Due Torri - the Asinelli Tower (97 meters, built around 1109) and the Garisenda Tower (48 meters, recorded as leaning as early as the 12th century) - are survivors of a group of more than 100 medieval towers once found in the city. These towers symbolized the power of Bologna's noble families. Dante mentions the Garisenda Tower in the Divine Comedy.

⏱ 75 min
3

Bologna's porticoes, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in July 2021, extend over 62 km throughout the city. Their origin dates to 1288, when a municipal law required all new construction to include an arcade to accommodate the influx of students to the world's oldest university, founded in 1088. The portico leading to the Sanctuary of San Luca, stretching 3.8 km with 664 arches, is the longest covered portico in the world.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Explore the Quadrilatero, the old quarter of medieval guilds, where stalls of charcuterie, cheeses, and fresh pasta line the arcades. Bologna, nicknamed La Grassa (the Fat One), is the birthplace of mortadella, tagliatelle al ragù, and tortellini.

🛏️ Suggested base : Firenze
★★★★★
1

Pisa is approximately 100 km from Florence, a 1-hour 20-minute drive. Stop at Pisa's Piazza dei Miracoli to see the Leaning Tower, the Baptistery and the Cathedral. Construction of the Leaning Tower of Pisa began in 1173, and it started leaning as soon as the third level was added because the clay soil was too soft on one side. Its construction took two centuries because of wars and interruptions. Stabilisation work between 1990 and 2001 reduced the tilt by 44 cm, returning it to the safe angle recorded in the early 19th century. Galileo is said to have conducted his experiments on falling bodies from the top - an anecdote that is probably apocryphal but firmly rooted in scientific legend.

Firenze

🛏️ Suggested base : Firenze
★★★★★
1

The Cathedral of Santa Maria del Fiore measures 153 meters long. Its dome, designed by Filippo Brunelleschi and built between 1420 and 1436, remains to this day the largest ever constructed in masonry: 45.5 meters in interior diameter, without a single wooden framework. Brunelleschi, trained as a goldsmith, invented unprecedented hoisting machines to lift materials. The interior of the dome is covered with Last Judgment frescoes begun by Vasari in 1572 and completed by Zuccari in 1579. Climbing the 463 steps to the summit provides 360° views across Florence.

⏱ 90 min
2

The Baptistery of San Giovanni, facing the Duomo, dates from the 11th century and ranks among Florence's oldest buildings. Its Gates of Paradise, created by Lorenzo Ghiberti between 1425 and 1452 in ten panels of gilded bronze depicting scenes from the Old Testament, were reportedly called the "gates of Paradise" by Michelangelo himself.

⏱ 45 min
3

The Ponte Vecchio, rebuilt in 1345 after an Arno flood, is the only bridge in Florence to survive World War II destruction—the retreating Germans in 1944 blew up all the others. Its shops have housed goldsmiths since 1593, when the Medici expelled the butchers whose smells offended their passage through the Corridoio Vasariano running above.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Stroll through the Oltrarno, the artisan quarter on the left bank of the Arno. Its lanes shelter furniture restoration workshops, leatherwork botteghe, and a neighborhood life less touristy than the center. Coffee on Piazza Santo Spirito in late afternoon.

Firenze

🛏️ Suggested base : Firenze
★★★★★
1

The Uffizi Gallery, commissioned by Cosimo I de' Medici in 1560 and designed by Vasari as administrative offices, opened to the public in 1769. Across 12,000 square meters and some 1,800 paintings, it concentrates the world's finest collection of Italian Renaissance art. The unmissables: Botticelli's Birth of Venus and Primavera, Leonardo da Vinci's Annunciation, Michelangelo's Doni Tondo (his only painting visible in Florence), and Titian's Venus of Urbino. In 1743, the last Medici heiress, Anna Maria Luisa, bequeathed the entire collection to Florence on condition that it never leave the city.

⏱ 150 min
2

The Accademia Gallery houses Michelangelo's David, carved between 1501 and 1504 from a 5.17-meter block of Carrara marble that two sculptors had previously abandoned. Michelangelo depicted David in the moment of concentration before battle, not in victory. The work stood in Piazza della Signoria until 1873, when it was moved indoors to protect it from weathering.

⏱ 75 min

Activities

Siena

🛏️ Suggested base : Siena
★★★★★
1

The Piazza del Campo, shell-shaped and divided into nine radiating sections—a symbolic tribute to the Council of Nine who governed Siena from 1287 to 1355—is one of Europe's most beautiful medieval squares. The Palazzo Pubblico (14th century), on the flat side, houses the Museo Civico with Ambrogio Lorenzetti's frescoes on Good and Bad Government (1338-1340), a political cycle unparalleled in Western medieval art. Twice a year, on July 2 and August 16, the square is covered with earth and ten horses representing the city's contrade compete in the Palio, a bareback race lasting about 90 seconds.

⏱ 75 min
2

The Torre del Mangia, built between 1325 and 1344, reaches 102 meters—exactly the same height as Siena's Duomo, asserting equality between civic and religious power. Its name comes from the nickname of its first bell-ringer, Giovanni di Duccio, called Mangiaguadagni ("earnings-eater"). At the tower's foot, the Cappella di Piazza was erected in 1352 by Sienese survivors of the Black Death as a sign of gratitude.

⏱ 60 min
3

The Duomo di Siena, built between 1215 and the 14th century in alternating white and green marble, is one of the finest examples of Italian Gothic. Its floor is unique in Italy: 56 panels of carved marble created by some forty artists over three centuries. The Piccolomini Library (1492), in the left nave, is decorated with frescoes by Pinturicchio depicting the life of Pope Pius II.

⏱ 75 min

Pienza

🛏️ Suggested base : Montepulciano · 9 km
★★★★☆
1

San Quirico d'Orcia, a fortified village in the Val d'Orcia (UNESCO World Heritage since 2004), was a major stop on the Via Francigena, the medieval route linking Canterbury to Rome. The Collegiata dei Santi Quirico e Giulitta, originating in the 8th century, opens through three portals of different styles—Lombard Romanesque, Gothic, Baroque—witnesses to successive reconstructions. The Horti Leonini, Italian gardens dating from 1581, overlook the surrounding hills.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Cipressi di San Quirico d'Orcia—two clusters of cypress trees isolated on hills along the Via Cassia—rank among Tuscany's most photographed landscapes. The Cappella della Madonna di Vitaleta, a small Renaissance chapel isolated between San Quirico and Pienza, is accessible by a dirt track and offers a backdrop of rolling hills and vineyards. Come at dawn or late afternoon.

⏱ 45 min
3

Pienza was a simple Tuscan village called Corsignano until 1462. Pope Pius II, born here in 1405, decided after his election to transform his birthplace into an "ideal city" according to Renaissance humanist principles. He entrusted the project to architect Bernardo Rossellino: in four years (1459-1462), a cathedral, papal palace, and central square were erected before the Pope's death halted construction. The Palazzo Piccolomini features hanging gardens on terraces overlooking the entire Orcia Valley.

Montepulciano

🛏️ Suggested base : Montepulciano
★★★★☆
1

Montepulciano stretches along a sandstone ridge at 605 meters elevation. Orvieto

🛏️ Suggested base : Orvieto
★★★★☆
1

The Duomo of Orvieto, whose first stone was laid in 1290 by Pope Nicholas IV, is one of Italy's most accomplished Gothic cathedrals. The reason for its construction: in 1263, a Bohemian priest doubting transubstantiation saw blood seep from a consecrated host at Bolsena. The stained corporal was brought to Orvieto, and the Pope ordered a cathedral built to house it. The facade, completed according to Lorenzo Maitani's plans from 1308, is a Gothic triptych covered in golden mosaics depicting the life of the Virgin. The bas-reliefs on the lower section rank among the finest from the Middle Ages.

⏱ 45 min
2

The Chapel of San Brizio (or Cappella Nova) is the interior masterpiece of the Duomo. Fra Angelico began decorating it in 1447—vaults only—before being recalled to Rome. Luca Signorelli took over in 1499 and covered walls and vaults with a cycle of frescoes on the Apocalypse and Last Judgment in two years. The preaching of the Antichrist, the Resurrection of the Flesh, and the damned cast into hell constitute an ensemble of expressive violence unprecedented in Italian art—and which directly inspired Michelangelo for the Sistine Chapel.

⏱ 45 min
3

St. Patrick's Well (Pozzo di San Patrizio) was commissioned by Pope Clement VII after the Sack of Rome in 1527, to ensure Orvieto's water supply in case of prolonged siege. Built by Antonio da Sangallo the Younger between 1527 and 1537, this 62-meter-deep well is an engineering masterpiece: two independent helical ramps of 248 steps each allowed laden mules to descend by one and ascend by the other without ever crossing paths. Seventy windows illuminate the descent.

⏱ 60 min

The Colosseum, whose construction began in 72 AD under Emperor Vespasian and was inaugurated in 80 under Titus, is the largest amphitheater ever built: 189 meters long, 156 meters wide, 48 meters high, with 80 arches, 80 entrances, and a capacity of approximately 50,000 spectators. Its underground—a network of galleries where gladiators and animals waited—was connected to the arena by trapdoors and hoisting systems. Gladiatorial combats, animal hunts, and public executions took place here for over four centuries.

⏱ 90 min
2

The Roman Forum and Palatine Hill, included with the Colosseum ticket, extend at the foot of the Palatine—site of Rome's legendary founding in 753 BC. The Forum was the civic, commercial, and religious heart of ancient Rome: temples, basilicas, triumphal arches, and the Sacred Way (Via Sacra) layer centuries of history upon each other. The Palatine Hill offers a panorama over the entire site.

⏱ 90 min
3

The Pantheon, built under Emperor Hadrian between 118 and 125 AD on the site of Agrippa's temple (27 BC), is the best-preserved Roman building in the world. Its unreinforced concrete dome measures 43.30 meters in diameter—exactly equal to its interior height. The central oculus of 8.7 meters is the sole source of light. On April 21, the mythical date of Rome's founding, the noon ray illuminates precisely the entrance portal. The painter Raphael is buried here, according to his last wishes, since 1520.

The Vatican Museums house one of the world's greatest art collections, amassed over 500 years of pontificates. The route passes through the Gallery of Maps (40 maps of 16th-century Italy), Raphael's Rooms (four rooms decorated by Raphael between 1509 and 1524, including the celebrated School of Athens) before culminating in the Sistine Chapel.

⏱ 120 min
2

The Sistine Chapel, built between 1477 and 1483 for Pope Sixtus IV (40 m long, 13 m wide, 21 m high), is famous for its frescoes. Michelangelo single-handedly painted the 500-square-meter ceiling between 1508 and 1512, commissioned by Julius II: nine scenes from Genesis including the Creation of Adam and some 350 figures. Twenty years later, he returned for the Last Judgment (1536-1541), a 13.7 × 14-meter fresco on the altar wall. The chapel serves as the conclave room for papal elections.

⏱ 30 min
3

St. Peter's Basilica, whose construction began in 1506 and concluded in 1626 on the site of St. Peter's tomb, is the largest Christian church in the world. Its successive architects include Bramante, Raphael, Michelangelo (who designed the dome, completed after his death in 1590), and Maderno. The dome measures 42 meters in diameter. St. Peter's Square, designed by Bernini between 1656 and 1667 with its two colonnades shaped like arms embracing the faithful, can hold up to 300,000 people.

Napoli

🛏️ Suggested base : Napoli
★★★★★
1

Pompeii is approximately 25 km from Naples, a 25-minute drive. The excavations of Pompeii form a striking archaeological site. In October AD 79, rather than August as was long believed - recent excavations point to 24-25 October - Vesuvius buried the city beneath several meters of ash and lapilli in less than 24 hours. Rediscovered by chance in 1748, the city covers 66 hectares, of which only three-fifths have been excavated. It contains 1,435 buildings, including 515 houses: baths, a forum, an amphitheater, a brothel, bakeries with millstones still in place and thermopolium counters used as street-food outlets. The plaster casts of victims, first made in 1861 by Giuseppe Fiorelli by injecting plaster into the voids left by the bodies, remain striking images from the site.

⏱ 180 min
2

Vesuvius, the only volcano in mainland Europe to have erupted in the past hundred years (last eruption: 1944), rises to 1,281 meters. In AD 79, the eruption column reached a height of 30 km. The volcano has been a national park since 1995. Access to the Gran Cono requires a ticket booked online; parking is lower down, followed by a shuttle to the trail entrance. The walk up provides views of the caldera and the Bay of Naples.

Amalfi

🛏️ Suggested base : Amalfi
★★★★★
1

The Cathedral of Sant'Andrea in Amalfi, built from 987 onward by Duke Mansone I, is the emblem of the ancient Maritime Republic of Amalfi—first of the four Italian Maritime Republics, which in the 10th-11th centuries controlled part of Mediterranean trade and counted approximately 70,000 inhabitants. It's accessed by a 57-step staircase from Piazza Duomo. The Arab-Norman facade blends golden mosaics, horseshoe arches, bicolor stripes, and ceramics. The bronze doors were cast in Constantinople in 1057—the first of their kind in Italy. The Cloister of Paradise (1266-1268), with its interlaced Moorish-influenced arches on twin columns, served as a cemetery for the Amalfitan nobility.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Arsenal of the Republic (Arsenale della Repubblica), a 12th-century shipyard preserved in its original state, houses the Museum of the Compass and Maritime Duchy. Amalfi's sailors were among the first in the West to use the compass for navigation. Around 1070, Amalfitan merchants established in Jerusalem founded the Hospital of Jerusalem—origin of the Knights Hospitaller of St. John.

Ravello is around a 20-minute drive from Amalfi. Ravello stands 365 meters above the Tyrrhenian Sea on a rocky promontory between Amalfi and Minori. Villa Rufolo, built in the 13th century by the Rufolo family - one of the most powerful families in the medieval Republic of Amalfi - combines Arab, Norman and Gothic influences in a hybrid architecture. Its belvedere overlooking the Bay of Salerno impressed Richard Wagner during his stay in 1880: he found inspiration there for Klingsor's magical garden in Parsifal. In his honor, Ravello hosts an international classical music festival in the villa's gardens every summer.

⏱ 60 min
2

Ravello is around a 20-minute drive from Amalfi. Villa Cimbrone, whose origins date back to the 11th century, was extensively transformed in the early 20th century by the British aristocrat Ernest William Beckett, who created gardens, terraces and a cloister combining Neo-Gothic, Neo-Moorish and Venetian styles. Its gardens, open to the public, stretch along the cliff edge. The Terrazza dell'Infinito, a balcony lined with marble busts at an altitude of 365 meters, offers a famous panorama over the Amalfi Coast: sea and sky merge on the horizon. Virginia Woolf, Greta Garbo, Winston Churchill and Gore Vidal stayed there.
