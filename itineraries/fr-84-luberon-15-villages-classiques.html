The 15 Most Beautiful Villages of the Luberon : 15 stops

Ansouis

🛏️ Suggested base : Lourmarin · 8 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Ansouis has been officially labeled one of France's Most Beautiful Villages since 1982—and the distinction is richly deserved. Its houses cling in a semicircle to the southern flank of a rocky spur facing the Mourre Nègre, the highest point of the Luberon. As you wander through its stone lanes (calades), be sure to visit the Balcon du Barri, the commanding panoramic viewpoint from the medieval ramparts overlooking the Lourmarin valley and the Luberon foothills. Claude Berri filmed several scenes from Jean de Florette and Manon des Sources here in 1986—and gazing at this landscape, you'll immediately understand why.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Chateau d'Ansouis has dominated the village since the 10th century. Originally a military fortress, with its square keep and four towers, it passed to the powerful Sabran family in 1178. The 17th century transformed it into a pleasure residence with some of the finest plasterwork decoration in Provence. It has been classified as a Historic Monument since 1948 and is still inhabited by its passionate owners, who personally conduct the guided tour. Book ahead: it is open from April through All Saints' Day, with weekend time slots. Allow at least 1.5 hours to appreciate the armoury hall, the terraced gardens, and the small 18th-century house known as the Hermitage, tucked away at the heart of the chateau.

⏱ 90 min
3

At Château Turcan, the Museum of Wine Arts and Trades displays over 3,000 tools related to viticulture, including one of Provence's oldest wine presses (18th century) and an oenology laboratory dating from 1900. A genuine curiosity, especially if you're interested in traditional winemaking techniques.

Lourmarin

🛏️ Suggested base : Lourmarin
★★★☆☆
1

Lourmarin is the southern gateway to the Luberon—and one of the region's most vibrant villages. Its cobbled lanes, plane-tree-shaded squares, and numerous art galleries make it both a heritage destination and a living community. This is where Albert Camus chose to settle in 1958 with his Nobel Prize money, purchasing an old silkworm farm that he furnished entirely as a gift to his family. He died on January 4, 1960, in a car accident while leaving the village; Lourmarin's footballers carried his coffin to the cemetery. You can reach it on foot in five minutes from the center: the grave, simple and covered with laurels, is one of the most visited in Provence.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Château de Lourmarin is often described as Provence's first Renaissance castle. Its construction began in the 15th century under the d'Agoult family and extended until 1560, featuring an elegant Italianate loggia. The Renaissance section—the only part open to the public—houses furnished apartments and stairways of surprising geometry. Each summer it also serves as the venue for the Summer Music Festival, whose starlit evening concerts are worth the trip in themselves.

⏱ 75 min
3

The village contains two symbolic religious buildings standing side by side: the Church of Saint-Andre and the Protestant Temple, a direct reflection of the Wars of Religion that marked the region. In 1545, a tragic episode unfolded here: the massacre of the Vaudois of the Luberon, during which the village was almost entirely destroyed before being repopulated by families from Piedmont and the Brianconnais.

⏱ 30 min

Lacoste

🛏️ Suggested base : Gordes · 12 km
★★★☆☆
1

Lacoste is one of the Luberon's most distinctive villages, and not only because of its most famous resident. Built on the flank of a limestone hill, its cobbled lanes climb among stone houses up to the summit esplanade, where the view embraces the Bonnieux plain and Mont Ventoux. The village owes its fame to the Marquis de Sade, whose family has owned the chateau since 1716. The author of The 120 Days of Sodom lived here several times to escape censorship and legal prosecution, notably from 1769 to 1772. The Revolution nearly destroyed it entirely, and Sade, learning the news in Paris, wrote: "What a loss! It is beyond expression. I am in despair!"

⏱ 60 min
2

The château, purchased in 2001, now hosts contemporary art exhibitions each summer and an opera festival in the adjoining quarries that have been transformed into a natural theater. The inner courtyard and grounds are open to visitors. From the summit, the panorama is exceptional: Bonnieux in the foreground, the Vaucluse Mountains behind, and on clear days the Alps on the horizon.

⏱ 60 min
3

As you descend the stone lanes, look for the galleries and artists' studios scattered throughout the village. Lacoste has attracted painters and sculptors for decades, drawn by the light and atmosphere—the ambiance is quite different from Gordes or Roussillon, more intimate and less touristy.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Climb to the castle plateau up to the summit viewpoint (20 min round trip from the center). The view over the Calavon valley and the Luberon ridges is one of the broadest in the northern Luberon.

⏱ 40 min

Walk along the stone lanes between terraces and dry-stone retaining walls, descending toward the old underground quarries that supplied the village's building stone. An impressive network of galleries, visible from certain access points along the path.

⏱ 50 min

Menerbes

🛏️ Suggested base : Gordes · 8 km
★★★☆☆
1

Ménerbes is one of the most evocative villages in the Luberon: a long rocky spur resembling a stone ship resting on the garrigue, it has remained in the world's imagination thanks to Peter Mayle, who wrote A Year in Provence there in 1989. But before Mayle, there was Picasso — who owned a house there — and Dora Maar, his photographer muse. As you wander through the lanes, you will still find the Maison Dora Maar, a beautiful 18th-century residence that has become an artists' residency and is open for visits on Tuesdays and Saturdays.

⏱ 75 min
2

The Maison de la Truffe et du Vin du Luberon has occupied the Hôtel d'Astier de Montfaucon since 2004, a 17th-century building classified as a Historic Monument on the Place de l'Horloge. You'll discover over 170 wine references from the Luberon appellation, sold at estate prices, and a shop specializing in truffle products. If you're here in winter, the small black truffle market (tuber melanosporum) on the last Sunday of December is an event in itself.

⏱ 60 min
3

The Tour de l'Horloge and the belfry of Ménerbes date from the 15th century. From the vaulted passage beside it, a view opens onto the plain and the neighbouring villages. Below the village on the road to Bonnieux, the Dolmen de la Pitchoune bears witness to human occupation since the Neolithic.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Loop walk along the viewpoints on the northern slope — views over the Petit Luberon and the Calavon plain.

⏱ 75 min

Descent toward the wine terraces at the foot of the village. The Côtes du Luberon appellation produces typical red and rosé wines here, and some estates welcome visitors for tastings. Call ahead before stopping by.

⏱ 60 min

Oppede

🛏️ Suggested base : Gordes · 11 km
★★★★☆
1

Oppede-le-Vieux is perhaps the most moving of the Luberon villages: half-ruined, half-restored, it feels frozen in the Middle Ages. The original village was gradually abandoned from the 20th century onward in favour of the lower village in the plain. In 1940, only six inhabitants were still holding out there. It was not until the 1960s that artists and heritage enthusiasts began restoring houses and stone lanes. Wear good shoes: the path from the car park climbs for 15 to 20 minutes along steep cobbled lanes before emerging onto the church esplanade.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Collégiale Notre-Dame-d'Alydon crowns the village. Its origins likely date to the 10th or 11th century, with significant alterations in the 16th and 17th centuries: the hexagonal bell tower dates from 1500-1501. The interior preserves an 18th-century gilded wooden altarpiece and a painting by Provençal artist Reynaud-Levieux (The Presentation of the Rosary). The building was saved from ruin through the efforts of a network of artists united by an annual festival. Free entry, irregular hours outside summer.

⏱ 45 min
3

The feudal castle ruins at the summit are inaccessible for safety reasons, but the silhouette is spectacular. A dark anecdote haunts this site: in 1545, Jean Maynier d'Oppède, lord of the château and First President of the Parliament of Aix, ordered the destruction of neighboring Waldensian villages, causing nearly 3,000 deaths. This massacre of the Vaudois of the Luberon remains one of the darkest episodes in Provençal history.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Climb via the stone lanes to the summit esplanade—360° view over the Calavon valley, Ventoux, and the Alps. The light is fantastic at midday on the honey-colored stones.

⏱ 45 min

Below the parking lot, the Jardins de Sainte-Cécile offer fifteen terraces of Mediterranean plants with over 80 hardy species. An unexpected natural respite at the foot of a medieval village.

⏱ 40 min

Goult

🛏️ Suggested base : Gordes · 6 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Goult is worth the effort: less known than Gordes or Roussillon, this village perched in the heart of the Luberon has retained genuine authenticity. Its château (private) belonged to the powerful Agoult family, whose name gave the village its identity. As you wander through its lanes with vaulted passages, arcades and old archways, you'll discover stone and ochre facades that change color with the light. The Thursday morning market on the Place de la Libération is a good excuse to meet local producers.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Moulin de Jerusalem is the village's crowning glory. Perched atop the hill behind the chateau, this late-17th-century windmill operated until 1919. It owes its unusual name to the surrounding quarter, so called in reference to the Crusades in which the lords of Agoult took part. After serving as an astronomy teaching centre for the Institut Geographique National from 1947 to 1990, it was restored to its original state by the municipality between 1994 and 1997. It still has its sails and its interior mechanism, a rarity in Provence. Panoramic view over the Calavon valley from the summit.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Heritage trail up to the windmill and the old communal oven (30 min from the center). The path is well marked and offers beautiful views over the Claparèdes plateau and the Luberon.

⏱ 45 min

Trail between dry-stone terraces, olive trees and vines—a short loop (approximately 1h) starting from the Conservatoire des terrasses de cultures, a restored landscape site showcasing local agricultural heritage. Make the detour to the Romanesque Chapel of Saint-Véran, nestled in a pine forest away from the village.

⏱ 60 min

Roussillon

🛏️ Suggested base : Gordes · 7 km
★★★★☆
1

Roussillon is unique in Europe: its facades display a palette of yellow, orange, red and violet that comes directly from the ochre in the subsoil. In 1780, a village resident named Jean Étienne Astier discovered that processed ochre becomes an indelible and non-toxic dye. The 19th century saw industrial exploitation reach its peak with 40,000 tons produced in 1929. Stroll through the village center lanes to understand how an entire community literally built itself from its own raw materials.

⏱ 75 min
2

The ochre cliffs and their quarry faces are spectacular from the viewpoints arranged at the village edge. The hues change according to the time of day—late afternoon light is the most photogenic. From the village, you have views over the surrounding pine forests and the Claparèdes plateau.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

The Sentier des Ocres (Ochre Trail) is unmissable. This circuit laid out through the old open-pit quarries offers two routes (30 or 50 minutes), marked with explanatory panels on the geology and history of extraction. The fairy chimneys and multicolored cliffs evoke the parks of the American West—without exaggeration. Arrive early in the morning to avoid crowds and enjoy the raking light on the ochres. In summer, plan on closed-toe shoes: the ochre sand stains soles and light-colored clothing.

⏱ 60 min

Walk through the pine forests and viewpoints over the Claparèdes plateau starting from the village (approximately 1h). The plateau offers an entirely different atmosphere from the quarries: wild garrigue, silence and distant views over the Luberon.

⏱ 60 min

Gordes

🛏️ Suggested base : Gordes
★★★★☆
1

Gordes is the postcard image of the Luberon—and for good reason. From below on the road climbing toward the village, the view of dry-stone houses terracing up the rocky spur is breathtaking. The château has dominated since 1031: originally a medieval fortress, it was profoundly remodeled in 1525 in Renaissance style by the d'Agoult-Simiane family, which ruled Gordes for seven centuries. Today the town hall and exhibition space, it's open to visitors. Plan to arrive early in the morning—the high season sees tour buses piling in from 9 AM onward.

⏱ 75 min
2

The Abbey of Sénanque is the second highlight. Founded in 1148 by Cistercian monks, it's one of the four Cistercian "sisters" of Provence. Nestled in the narrow Sénancole valley, it's still inhabited by a community of six Cistercian monks. From June to mid-July, the surrounding lavandin fields offer the spectacle of mauve lavender for which it's known worldwide. Outside this period, the atmosphere is even more serene. The guided tour (1h) is recommended. The access road is one-way from mid-March through late September.

⏱ 90 min
3

The Village des Bories, 4 km from the center, is a grouping of dry-stone dwellings without mortar—a technique inherited from the Bronze Age—which was used until the 19th century as seasonal shelter for farmers and shepherds. The twenty or so restored bories allow you to understand the organization of a traditional Provençal farm. Allow 45 minutes for the visit.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Loop around the ramparts and château esplanades early morning (20 min). From the esplanade below the château, you have the classic village view seen in every guidebook. Go early or late in the day for the light.

⏱ 30 min

Hiking loop: Abbey of Sénanque—Village des Bories from the village (9.5 km, approximately 2h30-3h, +270m elevation gain). One of the finest walks in the Luberon, linking Gordes' three flagship sites.

⏱ 180 min

Fontaine De Vaucluse

🛏️ Suggested base : Gordes · 6 km
★★★☆☆
1

Fontaine-de-Vaucluse gave its name to the department: "Vallis Clausa," the enclosed valley, attested as early as 979. The Sorgue spring that gushes at the foot of a 230-meter limestone cliff is France's most powerful resurgence, with an average annual flow of 630 million cubic meters. It's fed by waters from Mont Ventoux, the Albion plateau, and the Vaucluse Mountains. The first explorations of the chasm began in 1878; in 1985, divers reached a depth of -308 meters without finding the bottom. The walk from the village to the chasm takes 15 to 20 minutes on foot. The view is most spectacular in spring, when the waters boil at high flow.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Musee-Bibliotheque Francois Petrarque, on the left bank of the Sorgue, is dedicated to the Tuscan poet who settled in Fontaine-de-Vaucluse from 1338 onward to escape the tumult of the papal court in Avignon. Here he wrote much of his Canzoniere, 366 poems dedicated to Laure de Sade, who died of the Plague in 1348. The museum also exhibits a modern art collection bringing together names such as Braque, Picasso, Miro and Giacometti, all connected to the work of the poet Rene Char, a native of L'Isle-sur-la-Sorgue.

⏱ 60 min
3

The Church of Saint-Véran, classified as a Historic Monument since 1840, is built on the remains of a Roman temple. It's one of the oldest in the diocese of Cavaillon, with an 11th-century Romanesque nave. It houses the sarcophagus of Saint Véran, 6th-century bishop of Cavaillon, reputed to have slain a legendary beast—the Coulobre—in the Sorgue waters.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Round trip to the chasm from the parking lot (15-20 min walk each way). The path follows the Sorgue and its waterwheels. Note: access has been reduced for safety reasons (possible rockfall), the observation point has been moved 50 meters upstream.

⏱ 60 min

Trail along the left bank of the Sorgue, shaded section under the plane trees (45 min). The emerald green waters of the Sorgue allow kayaking and aquatic hiking—providers offer these activities from the village.

⏱ 45 min

Murs

🛏️ Suggested base : Gordes · 7 km
★★☆☆☆
1

At 500 meters altitude in the Vaucluse Mountains, Murs is a welcome antidote to the tourist bustle of Gordes, just 8 km away. Its château has dominated the village since the 12th century—originally built by the d'Agoult family, expanded between the 15th and 18th centuries. It's private and not open to visitors, but the facade with its round towers, weathered walls, and wrought-iron gate bearing the Astouaud coat of arms merits a stop. The village is also the birthplace of Le Brave Crillon, Henri IV's companion in arms, born in 1543 during his mother's visit to the lord of Murs. His 13th-century house is still visible in the lanes.

⏱ 75 min
2

Stroll through the village center lanes to spot the fountains with unusual names (fox fountain, sheep fountain) and the numerous bories catalogued by the Luberon Park. On the northern edge of the territory, the Mur de la Peste is a 25-km dry-stone sanitary line built in four months in 1721 to stop the epidemic coming from Marseille. A marked hiking trail of 7.5 km follows it.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Ridge loop toward the valleys and isolated farms of the karst plateau. The Gorges de Véroncle, a few kilometers away, are an exceptional natural site with ruined watermills and waterfalls—a hike off the usual tourist paths (2h round trip).

⏱ 120 min

Circuit of oratories on the edge of the garrigue (approximately 1h). Murs has several old oratories and wayside crosses scattered in the countryside, witnesses to preserved rural heritage.

⏱ 60 min

Saint Saturnin Les Apt

🛏️ Suggested base : Saignon · 9 km
★★★☆☆
1

Saint-Saturnin-lès-Apt is a medieval town that underwent three successive expansions in the 13th, 15th, and 17th centuries, each leaving its traces in the urban fabric. As you climb the cobbled lanes toward the château, you'll cross the remains of several fortified enclosures. The Portail Eyguier, a 15th-century corner tower, is the sole survivor of the second enclosure. From the summit ramparts, the panorama embraces the Alpilles, the Vaucluse Mountains, and both Luberon massifs—a 180° view over all of Provence. In the village center lanes, look for the carved doors and window frames classified as Historic Monuments, as well as the 18th-century public fountain whose sculpted pediment was a gift from the last Marquis de Monclar—guillotined in 1794.

⏱ 75 min
2

The Castle Chapel, consecrated in 1056, is perched atop the original rock on the remains of a 6th-century early Christian church. Accessible on request at the town hall, it's worth the detour. In 1850, a villager named Rosette Tamisier reportedly saw a painting bleed here—the affair caused quite a stir before she was imprisoned for fraud. Two 17th-century windmills dominate the hill, one of which still has its sails—a rarity in Provence.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Climb to the castle plateau via the cobbled lanes (20 min round trip). View from the orientation table over the Apt basin and the ochres of Roussillon on the horizon.

⏱ 40 min

Trail of the Aiguiers—a discovery loop around the cisterns carved into the limestone rock to collect rainwater. Allow 1h30 for a short loop. A rare hydraulic heritage, well explained by the Luberon Park panels.

⏱ 90 min

Villars

🛏️ Suggested base : Saignon · 8 km
★★★☆☆
1

Villars sits on an isolated hillock between the Vaucluse Mountains and the Grand Luberon, 8 km from Apt. The village's establishment dates back to the late 11th century; a 12th or 13th-century square defensive tower formed the core of the castrum around which it developed. The commune's territory includes twelve hamlets corresponding to the old Gallo-Roman villæ. It's a characterful village that has preserved its authenticity: fountain, shaded squares, and typically Provençal stone houses.

⏱ 60 min
2

The old center preserves traces of the fortified town—arched passages and narrow lanes—and some fine 16th and 17th-century houses. The Château Saint-Pons wine estate in Villars offers a marked vineyard trail around its parcels in the AOC Ventoux appellation.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Rural walk toward the hillsides and dry-stone walls—shepherds' shelters and bories dot the paths around the village. The local economy still cultivates cherry trees (cœurs de pigeon and griottes), apricots and lavender: the landscapes change with the seasons.

⏱ 60 min

Photography route at sunset over the Claparèdes plateau—the raking light on the garrigue and lavandin fields is particularly beautiful in June-July. The plateau overlooks the Calavon valley and extends toward the Colorado Provençal de Rustrel, 5 km away.

Caseneuve

🛏️ Suggested base : Saignon · 7 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Caseneuve is one of the most discreet villages in the eastern Luberon—and that's precisely what makes it precious. Perched at 555 meters altitude, it overlooks the Apt valley with an unbeatable view of the Luberon massif. The site has been inhabited since Roman colonization. The present château, a towering 32-meter fortress with ramparts, great towers and high weathered stone walls, took its place between the 10th and 12th centuries under the lords of Agoult-Simiane, who held it for centuries. First mentioned in 978, Caseneuve was never attacked—only the Plague of 1720-21 claimed eighteen victims among its inhabitants. The château is private and not open to visitors, but its silhouette is impressive.

⏱ 75 min
2

At the village entrance, the Saint-Jean oratory is a curiosity not to be missed: built in 1830 after a Franciscan mission, it's the largest oratory in Provence. Its pointed-arch facade with columns and pilasters gives it a deceptive Romanesque appearance. From the forecourt, there's an open view over the Luberon countryside. In the village, the 17th-century Maison Commune (former seat of the Town Council) and the Church of Saint-Étienne complete the heritage picture.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Loop on the plateau edge (approximately 1h). Moors and lavandin fields depending on the season, with open views over the Luberon range. The Colorado Provençal de Rustrel, just 4 km away, makes an ideal afternoon excursion from Caseneuve.

⏱ 60 min

Spotting the old drailles (drove roads) and clapas (stone piles used to mark parcels or shelter shepherds) in the landscape around the village. Caseneuve is part of the Luberon Geological Nature Reserve—the subsoil regularly reveals vestiges of intense Gallo-Roman colonization.

Viens

🛏️ Suggested base : Saignon · 14 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Viens celebrated its millennium in 1990, and the village wears its age well. On the edge of the Alpes-de-Haute-Provence, it grew up around a fortress built around 990, first mentioned in 1005 under the name "Vegnis." Enter the village through the Porte Sarrasine (also called the Tour de la Pousterle), pierced in the ramparts of the 11th to 13th centuries. It allows only one person through at a time, and its crown of machicolations was partially destroyed around 1960. The Clock Tower above it, with its square belfry, belongs to the 14th-century enclosure. In the narrow lanes, you will come across fine dwellings from the 13th to the 16th centuries with mullioned and transomed windows. A Rue de la Juiverie recalls that a Jewish community found refuge here in the 14th century.

⏱ 75 min
2

Viens' Renaissance château (16th century, renovated in the 18th and 19th centuries) now serves as the village town hall. Passing through its portal, you'll discover a magnificent octagonal Renaissance staircase—the most remarkable architectural piece in the village. The town hall opens its doors during the day. The Church of Saint-Hilaire (12th century, remodeled in the 17th) is listed as a Historic Monument for its late-13th-century square bell tower with twin bays and Lombard bands.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Walk around the ramparts and viewpoint over the Albion plateau from the Montée de la Pousterle (20 min). The view stretches across the Luberon massif.

⏱ 30 min

Rural path toward the terraces and fields (short 1h loop). The surroundings of Viens are located on the border of the Alpes-de-Haute-Provence, with lavender fields beginning to appear on the surrounding plateaus.

Saignon

🛏️ Suggested base : Saignon
★★★☆☆
1

Saignon magnificently concludes this tour of the Luberon. This eagle's nest above Apt takes its name from the Latin "signum"—the rock dominating the village served as a light signal for Gallic populations before the Romans arrived. From the Place de la Fontaine, with its centuries-old hackberry tree and cool fountains, wander through narrow, steep lanes where every doorway deserves a glance. The village counted up to forty different lords in the 14th century—which is to say, a tumultuous history.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Church of Notre-Dame-de-Pitié dates back to the 11th and 12th centuries. Classified as a Historic Monument, this Romanesque church houses Carolingian baptismal fonts, red marble columns, and a 16th-century reliquary. In the Middle Ages, it was a stop on the Via Domitia for pilgrims en route to Santiago de Compostela. The portal with three doors and its large Gothic arch is particularly beautiful.

⏱ 30 min
3

The Rocher de Bellevue is the village's highest point—about thirty meters above the houses. It served as the base for three medieval fortress-châteaux whose ramparts and sentry paths are still visible. An orientation table at the summit allows you to identify Mont Ventoux, the Montagne de Lure, and the Alps.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Climb to the Rocher de Bellevue via the fitted-out stairs (10 min from the village). 360° view over the Apt valley, the Luberon, Ventoux, and on clear days the Alps. The most beautiful panoramic ending imaginable for an itinerary.

⏱ 40 min

Circuit of the two wash-houses fed by the Valsorgues springs, and of the bories and clapas that mark the paths around the village. The Claparèdes Plateau trail connects Saignon to Sivergues through a landscape of garrigue and dry stone.

⏱ 60 min
