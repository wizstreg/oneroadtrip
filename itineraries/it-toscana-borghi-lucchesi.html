Lucca Province Circuit: Villages and Coast : 14 stops

Lucca

🛏️ Suggested base : Lucca
★★★★☆
1

Piazza dell'Anfiteatro: Lucca's oval plaza is no reconstructed set piece—it traces the exact perimeter of a 2nd-century AD Roman amphitheater that could hold 10,000 spectators. Medieval residents built their houses against the stone tiers, erasing the arena but preserving its elliptical form. The 19th-century restoration by architect Lorenzo Nottolini cleared the square and opened the four arched entrances that still produce the element of surprise described by Grand Tour travelers. Enter through one of the vaulted passages to understand how an entire city grew on the bones of a Roman monument.

⏱ 45 min
2

San Martino Cathedral: Founded in the 6th century by Bishop Fridian, rebuilt from the 11th century onward, and completed in the 16th–17th centuries, the cathedral displays a distinctive asymmetrical façade: the architect had to narrow the final arcade to fit the pre-existing bell tower, creating an elegant dissonance. Inside, the main treasure is the Volto Santo, a 13th-century wooden crucifix traditionally attributed to Nicodemus and honored with a torchlit procession every September 13 since the Middle Ages. In the sacristy, the tomb of Ilaria del Carretto (1406) sculpted by Jacopo della Quercia shows the young wife of Paolo Guinigi lying with a dog at her feet—a symbol of marital fidelity—in a style that would influence Donatello.

⏱ 60 min
3

Torre Guinigi: The Guinigi family's red brick tower, erected in the late 14th century, reaches 44.25 meters. Its unique feature is a garden of holm oaks planted at the summit—a symbol of rebirth chosen by the Guinigi, then lords of Lucca. Paolo Guinigi, who ruled the city from 1400 to 1430, made this tower the emblem of his mercantile and banking power. Climb the 230 steps for sweeping views of tiled rooftops, ramparts, and, on clear days, the Apuan Alps.

⏱ 60 min
4

San Michele in Foro Church: Built on the site of the ancient Roman forum, San Michele displays a 12th-century Pisan-Lucchese façade with four tiers of sculpted colonnettes, each different from the next. Throughout the Middle Ages, the building also served as the courthouse for the Republic of Lucca. Inside: a 14th-century frescoed Virgin and Child and a glazed ceramic Madonna by Luca della Robbia.

⏱ 45 min
5

Puccini's Birthplace (Museo Puccini): Giacomo Puccini was born on December 22, 1858, in this apartment on Corte San Lorenzo, where the Puccini family had served for five generations as organists and choirmasters at the cathedral. The museum preserves the Steinway & Sons piano purchased in 1901 on which he composed Turandot, opera manuscripts, costumes, and stage photographs. Puccini bought back the family apartment after his sister sold it—proof of the composer's attachment to his native city.

Activities

Bike ride on the ramparts: Built between 1513 and 1650 along a 4.2 km circuit, Lucca's Renaissance walls constitute one of the best-preserved examples of modern fortifications in Europe. Thirty meters wide at the base and 12 meters high, they never saw combat—Lucca maintained its independence through diplomacy until 1799. Today they're transformed into a tree-lined promenade bordered by plane trees and oaks, accessible on foot or by bike. Rentals are available at the main gates.

Lucca

🛏️ Suggested base : Lucca
★★★★☆
1

Palazzo Mansi - National Museum: Built in the 17th century for a wealthy family of Lucchese merchants, this palace preserves its state apartments almost intact—music room with allegorical frescoes, hall of mirrors, painting collections from the Medici and Grand Duchy holdings. Lucca built its fortune between the 13th and 15th centuries on silk trade, exporting fabrics as far as England and Flanders. This palace is the direct architectural expression of that wealth.

⏱ 60 min
2

Palazzo Pfanner and Gardens: Built in 1660 for the Moricani family, then purchased in the mid-19th century by the Pfanner family of Austrian origin—who still own it. It opens onto one of the few Italian-style gardens still preserved within the walls, with a central fountain surrounded by statues of deities and seasons. Jane Campion's film The Portrait of a Lady was partly shot here.

⏱ 45 min
3

Via Fillungo and Historic Center: The main thoroughfare of the pedestrian center is lined with historic cafés, artisan shops, and fragments of medieval architecture. The Torre delle Ore, whose first clock dates to 1390, marks its northern end. Puccini frequented Caffè Di Simo, an establishment opened in 1875 whose Art Nouveau storefront is still visible.

⏱ 60 min
4

Villa Guinigi - National Museum: Former country residence of Paolo Guinigi, lord of Lucca from 1400 to 1430, built outside the medieval walls in the style of Venetian Palladian villas with an arcaded loggia. The museum houses collections ranging from 8th-century BC Etruscan finds to medieval sculptures from churches suppressed during the Napoleonic era.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Antiques market: Every third Sunday of the month, the antiques market spreads across the main squares of the historic center—Piazza San Martino, Piazza San Giovanni, Piazza Bernardini—with several hundred vendors. It's one of the most frequented antiques markets in Tuscany.

Montecarlo

🛏️ Suggested base : Bagni Di Lucca · 19 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Rocca di Cerruglio: The village of Montecarlo owes its name to Charles IV of Luxembourg—Mons Charoli in Latin—who contributed in 1333 to liberating Lucca from Pisan occupation. The Republic of Lucca then had the destroyed village rebuilt on Cerruglio hill and erected this three-tower fortress: the semi-circular keep, the Tower of the Apparition, and the Tower of Saint Barbara, the latter named for a legend that the Virgin appeared to defend the village during a 15th-century Pisan siege. The fortress is open to visitors with views over the plain of Lucca and the Valdinievole.

⏱ 60 min
2

Collegiata di Sant'Andrea: In the heart of the village, this collegiate church founded in the 14th century preserves its original Gothic façade and portal, remodeled in Baroque style in the 18th century. Inside, a fresco of the 15th-century Florentine school depicts the Madonna del Soccorso snatching a child from the devil's claws. Two plaques commemorate visits by poet Giuseppe Ungaretti in the 1960s and composer Giacomo Puccini.

⏱ 40 min
3

Teatro dei Rassicurati: Built in the early 18th century, this tiny Italian-style theater ranks among the five smallest in Italy. It takes its name from the Academy of the Rassicurati, a local learned society founded in 1690. Puccini visited several times. The auditorium, with its lacquered wooden boxes and ceiling frescoes, has remained almost unchanged since construction.

⏱ 30 min
4

Pieve di San Piero in Campo: At the foot of the hill, this 12th-century Romanesque parish church with a nave and two aisles is one of the oldest Christian buildings in Lucca province. Its sober limestone façade and austere interior contrast with the Baroque exuberance of the collegiate church perched at the top of the village.

⏱ 30 min

Borgo A Mozzano

🛏️ Suggested base : Bagni Di Lucca · 8 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Ponte della Maddalena (known as Ponte del Diavolo): This humpback bridge spans the Serchio with a large central arch 18.50 meters high flanked by four smaller asymmetric arches—a silhouette that has intrigued travelers since the 16th century, notably Michel de Montaigne who described it in his Journal de Voyage of 1581. The structure probably dates to the 11th century, but its current appearance is due to Castruccio Castracani, lord of Lucca, who had it rebuilt between 1324 and 1327. On the Via Francigena, the medieval pilgrimage route from Canterbury to Rome, the bridge survived World War II: retreating German troops had mined it but it was not destroyed, which proved crucial for the Allies. Legend has it that the mason, driven to despair by the Serchio's floods, made a pact with the devil to finish the work in one night—in exchange for the first soul to cross the bridge. The village priest sent a dog across, outwitting the devil. They say this white dog still walks the bridge on October evenings.

Bagni Di Lucca

🛏️ Suggested base : Bagni Di Lucca
★★☆☆☆
1

Historic Spa Center and Terme Bagni di Lucca: Known since Roman times, Bagni di Lucca's hot springs began to flourish in the 11th century thanks to Countess Matilda of Canossa, and the town became one of Europe's most fashionable spa resorts in the 19th century. Elisa Baciocchi, Napoleon's sister and Princess of Lucca, had the Bernabò spa built here in 1810, where waters emerge naturally at 39.2°C. The spa complex has welcomed Byron, Shelley, Montale, Henry James, and Puccini. The English called the place “the Switzerland of Tuscany.”

⏱ 60 min
2

Real Casino de Ponte a Serraglio: Inaugurated in 1839, this casino was one of the very first in Europe—some sources credit Bagni di Lucca with inventing roulette around 1837. The Art Nouveau building, restored and reopened in 2009 after years of closure, overlooks the banks of the Lima and is one of the best-preserved reminders of 19th-century aristocratic tourism.

⏱ 30 min
3

Ponte delle Catene: this iron suspension bridge designed by Lorenzo Nottolini - the same architect who remodeled the Piazza dell'Anfiteatro in Lucca - is one of Italy's oldest metal bridges still standing. It spans the Lima.

⏱ 20 min
4

Italy's first Anglican church and English cemetery: The British community established in Bagni di Lucca from the early 19th century built the country's first Anglican church here. The adjoining cemetery holds the graves of English and American residents, as well as residents of other nationalities, who chose to end their days in this valley.

⏱ 20 min

Activities

Hiking in the Orrido di Botri: plan a day trip from Bagni di Lucca, traveling by road to the canyon entrance and then continuing on foot. This canyon, carved by the Pelago stream in the Apennines, offers sheer limestone gorges and waterfalls. Access is regulated, and visiting with a local guide is preferable.

⏱ 180 min

Thermal treatments: The Bagni Caldi thermal establishments offer pools, natural steam grottos, and treatments using sulfurous waters whose natural temperature approaches 39°C.

Castelnuovo Di Garfagnana

🛏️ Suggested base : Castelnuovo Di Garfagnana
★★★☆☆
1

Rocca Ariostesca: This trapezoidal fortress has dominated Piazza Umberto I since the 10th century, but its 14th-century reconstruction by Castruccio Castracani gave it its current form. It takes its name from poet Ludovico Ariosto—author of Orlando Furioso—who resided here from 1522 to 1525 as governor of the Este province on behalf of Alfonso I d'Este, Duke of Ferrara. Ariosto, who called Garfagnana “land of wolves and brigands,” nevertheless wrote several of his Satires here. The central tower, equipped with a civic clock, and the terrace added in 1675 overlooking the square make it one of the most complete examples of municipal fortification in the region. Every Thursday, the weekly market is held at the foot of the fortress—a tradition so ancient that a ducal decree once forbade arresting criminals on that day.

⏱ 60 min
2

Cathedral of Santi Pietro e Paolo: Built in the 15th century in Renaissance style on an 11th-century Romanesque church, then largely remodeled in the Baroque era. The austere gray stone façade contrasts with the richness of the interior: apse redone in the 19th century, Baroque altars, and series of 15th–16th century paintings. The church is a waypoint on the Via Matildica del Volto Santo linking Mantua to Lucca.

⏱ 30 min
3

Medieval Historic Center and ramparts: The fortified heart of Castelnuovo, at the confluence of the Serchio and Turrite Secca, preserves its medieval alleys, arched gates (Porta dei Borselli), and Clock Tower. The town is the administrative capital of Garfagnana and the meeting point of two historic pilgrimage routes.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Sentiero dell'Ariosto to Fortezza di Mont'Alfonso: A 6 km hiking trail dedicated to the poet, starting from Rocca Ariostesca, crossing the bridge over the Turrite Secca and climbing toward the village of Torrite then the bastioned fortress of Mont'Alfonso, built by Alfonso II d'Este between 1579 and 1586, with panoramas over the Apuan Alps and Apennines.

⏱ 180 min

Thursday weekly market: Castelnuovo's market is the most important in Garfagnana, with cheeses, charcuterie (biroldo della Garfagnana, Colonnata lard), and mountain products.

⏱ 90 min

Barga

🛏️ Suggested base : Barga
★★★☆☆
1

Duomo di San Cristoforo: Perched at the village's highest point (410 m), this Romanesque cathedral was built in stages from the 11th to 16th centuries in blocks of alberese, a local gray-cream limestone. Its 13th-century marble pulpit, the work of Guido Bigarelli, is supported by four columns, two resting on recumbent lions. Inside: a tabernacle for the Holy Oils by Andrea della Robbia, a 14th-century Madonna del Molino, and a polychrome medieval wooden statue of San Cristoforo. From the forecourt, views extend over the entire historic center and, on clear days, to the Apuan Alps.

⏱ 60 min
2

Palazzo Pretorio and Museo Civico del Territorio: Residence of Florentine commissioners from 1341 to 1859, following Barga's decision to submit to Florence rather than Lucca, which secured exemption from numerous taxes. The palace houses the municipal museum with archaeological collections (prehistory, Ligurians, Romans) and artworks from the Middle Ages and Renaissance. The old medieval prisons are still open to visitors.

⏱ 45 min
3

Loggia dei Mercanti and Piazza Salvo Salvi: This loggia with slender columns was built in 1546 on orders from Cosimo I de' Medici to shelter Barga's market, whose main products were silk and salt. The stone lion (Marzocco) on the façade symbolizes Florence's political guardianship. Palazzo Pancrazi, which houses the town hall, displays a façade with diamond-point rustication characteristic of 16th-century Florentine architecture.

⏱ 30 min
4

Teatro dei Differenti: Inaugurated in 1689 and rebuilt in 1795, this Italian-style theater is one of the best preserved in Tuscany. It was here that Giovanni Pascoli delivered on November 26, 1911, his famous speech La grande proletaria si è mossa in favor of the Italo-Turkish War in Libya.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Barga Jazz Festival (August): Since 1986, this internationally renowned jazz festival has brought the squares and alleys of the medieval historic center to life each summer. Musicians from around the world perform in this medieval setting for a week in August.

1

Casa Museo Giovanni Pascoli in Castelvecchio (4 km from Barga): Giovanni Pascoli, a major poet of Italian Symbolism, purchased this 18th-century country house in 1895 and lived here with his sister Mariù until his death in 1912. He composed his most celebrated collections here, including Myricae and Canti di Castelvecchio, directly inspired by the surrounding landscapes. The museum preserves books, manuscripts, and everyday objects as the family left them. Pascoli is buried in the garden chapel, beneath cypresses and wisteria.

Enter through Porta Reale—so renamed after Grand Duke Leopold of Lorraine's visit—and climb the cobbled alleys to the Duomo. The presence of a significant Scottish community, descended from hundreds of people from Barga who left to settle in Glasgow between the 19th and 20th centuries as ice cream vendors and fish and chips shop managers, has earned Barga the nickname “most Scottish town in Italy.”

Coreglia Antelminelli

🛏️ Suggested base : Barga · 4 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Museum of Plaster Figurines and Emigration (Palazzo Vanni): from the 16th to the 20th century, the figurinai of Coreglia Antelminelli made plaster statuettes - religious subjects, mythological figures and busts of famous people - and traveled to sell them in the major cities of Europe and beyond. This migration led to the founding in 1975 of the museum, which now houses 1 300 pieces divided into two sections: one devoted to works from the 17th-20th centuries, the other to the artisans' emigration, with passports, letters and documents from the period. Among the most unusual pieces are 18th-century cats colored with candle smoke.

⏱ 60 min
2

San Michele Arcangelo Church and Tower of Castruccio Castracani: Built in the 12th century on the site of an old Antelminelli fortress, San Michele church preserves an Annunciation from the Pisan school (1352), a 14th-century painting of the Archangel Saint Michael, and a pre-Romanesque ambo depicting a cow. The imposing adjoining bell tower—former watchtower of Castruccio Castracani, erected in the 11th century and approximately 25 meters high—dominates the village from the ridge at 595 meters altitude.

⏱ 45 min
3

Historic Center and panorama over the Serchio valley: The medieval village, listed among the “Borghi più belli d'Italia,” preserves its vaulted streets, arched gates, and 16th-century palaces (Palazzo Antonimi, Palazzo Vincenti, town hall dating from 1572). From the square outside the walls, views extend over the entire Serchio valley and the silhouette of the Apuan Alps to the west.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Medieval Day on August 15th: Every August 15th, Coreglia Antelminelli organizes a historical reenactment with a procession of halberdiers, artisan market, tastings of local products, and street performances in the medieval village lit by torches.

Camaiore

🛏️ Suggested base : Forte Dei Marmi · 11 km
★★★☆☆
1

Collegiata Santa Maria Assunta: Founded in the 13th century and profoundly remodeled in the Baroque era, this collegiate church is Camaiore's central monument. Its Romanesque façade contrasts with the decorative richness of the Baroque interior. The adjoining sacred art museum collections preserve furnishings, objects, and paintings from the 14th–16th centuries, including several noteworthy works of local provenance.

⏱ 45 min
2

San Pietro Abbey: Founded by Benedictine monks between the 8th and 13th centuries, this Romanesque abbey with a nave, two aisles, and a bell tower is one of the oldest surviving examples of Christianity in Versilia. Accessible on foot from the center via an olive-lined path, it is entered through a 14th-century gate that retains part of the original monastery walls. The sober architecture in local limestone reflects Benedictine austerity.

⏱ 40 min
3

Pieve dei Santi Giovanni e Stefano: Erected in the 12th century at the junction of the Via Francigena (of which Camaiore is the 26th stage), this Romanesque parish church has a bell tower with paired windows on three levels. Inside, the baptismal font was carved from a 3rd-century BC Roman sarcophagus—an example of reuse characteristic of medieval religious architecture.

⏱ 30 min
4

Archaeological Museum of Palazzo Tori Massoni: Housed in a Renaissance palace, this museum preserves finds covering the prehistoric, Ligurian, Etruscan, Roman, and medieval periods. The Camaiore region (its name derived from the Roman Campus Major, the great plain) has been inhabited since the Neolithic, as attested by finds from the woods of Lucese.

⏱ 40 min

Activities

Hiking in the hills of Versilia around Camaiore: The hills overlooking the town toward the Apuan Alps offer routes through vineyards, olive groves, and hilltop villages like Monteggiori, Casoli, or Gombitelli, with views of the sea and countryside. Camaiore is an ideal starting point for hikes toward Monte Prana.

Pietrasanta

🛏️ Suggested base : Forte Dei Marmi · 5 km
★★★☆☆
1

Piazza del Duomo and Duomo San Martino: Pietrasanta's rectangular square is the artistic heart of the town. San Martino Cathedral, whose construction dates to the 14th century, presents a façade clad in local marble and an interior enriched with 19th-century frescoes and artworks from preceding centuries. The brick bell tower, commissioned around 1520 from Donato Benti—a collaborator of Michelangelo—conceals an extraordinary self-supporting helical staircase in brick, long overlooked. It was during the period when Michelangelo stayed in Pietrasanta to extract marble destined for the façade of San Lorenzo in Florence that this staircase was designed, according to most historians.

⏱ 60 min
2

Museo dei Bozzetti (Museum of Maquettes) - Chiostro di Sant'Agostino: Created in 1984 in the former convent of Sant'Agostino (14th century), this one-of-a-kind museum documents the creative process of more than 350 Italian and international sculptors who have worked in Pietrasanta. It preserves over 700 maquettes and plaster models that preceded marble or bronze works now dispersed in museums and public spaces worldwide. Botero, Mitoraj, Folon, Cascella, Pomodoro, Miró, and Henry Moore are represented.

⏱ 60 min
3

Marble and bronze workshops - Parco Internazionale della Scultura Contemporanea: Pietrasanta houses some fifty marble sculpture workshops and art bronze foundries. The town, nicknamed “Little Athens,” has welcomed sculptors from around the world since the 15th century, when Michelangelo stayed here to work marble from nearby quarries. Henry Moore was among the first modern foreign artists to discover Pietrasanta in the 1950s; he was followed by Noguchi, Miró, Mitoraj, and Botero. The International Park of Contemporary Sculpture presents over 70 monumental works donated to the town, visible in the streets and squares.

⏱ 90 min
4

Museo Archeologico Bruno Antonucci (Palazzo Moroni, 16th century): Located on Piazza del Duomo, this museum presents collections ranging from prehistory to the medieval era, with Etruscan and Roman pieces discovered in the Versilia territory.

⏱ 40 min

Activities

Stroll among art galleries and sculptors' workshops: Pietrasanta has more than one gallery per 1,200 inhabitants—a record density even in Tuscany. Several workshops open their doors to the public, allowing visitors to observe artisans working on marble or bronze.

⏱ 120 min

Weekly Thursday market and antiques market (1st Sunday of the month): the Thursday market offers food, crafts and local products. The antiques market takes place on the first Sunday of the month.

⏱ 90 min

Forte Dei Marmi

🛏️ Suggested base : Forte Dei Marmi
★★★☆☆
1

Fortino (Fort Lorenz): At the center of Piazza Garibaldi, this quadrangular fort was built in the late 16th century on orders of Grand Duke Pietro Leopoldo of Tuscany, initially to protect the coastline and store marble from the Apuan Alps before loading it onto ships. The town takes its name from this fort—Forte dei Marmi literally means “fort of the marbles.” Until April 26, 1914, Forte dei Marmi was part of Pietrasanta; it became an independent municipality on that date. The fort today houses the Museo della Satira e della Caricatura, founded on the collections of the Political Satire Prize created in 1973.

⏱ 45 min
2

Pontile (historic pier): Built between 1867 and 1877 to load marble from the Apuan Alps directly onto ships, this wharf equipped with rails and a steam crane was destroyed during World War II and rebuilt in 1955. The walk on the jetty, with the Apuan Alps behind and the Tyrrhenian Sea ahead, is the emblematic panorama of Forte dei Marmi.

⏱ 30 min
3

Parco della Versiliana: On the border between Forte dei Marmi and Marina di Pietrasanta, this park of umbrella pines spanning several dozen hectares was frequented by literary and artistic figures of the 19th and 20th centuries. It now hosts a summer cultural festival (theater, concerts, exhibitions) renowned throughout Italy.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Beach and swimming in Versilia: The beach clubs of Forte dei Marmi, with their white-striped tents and wooden changing cabins, are set on a fine sandy beach about 5 km long. The sandy seabed slopes gently into the Tyrrhenian Sea.

⏱ 180 min

Excursion to the marble quarries of Carrara: About 20 km north, the quarries of the Apuan Alps have been worked since Roman times. Michelangelo came to personally select his blocks. Guided tours in all-terrain vehicles allow visitors to get close to active quarry faces.

⏱ 180 min
