Aosta Valley Circuit: From Roman Gateway to Mont Blanc : 8 stops

Pont Saint Martin

Suggested base : Chatillon · 22 km
★★★☆☆
1

The Roman bridge at Pont-Saint-Martin, built around the 1st century BC under Augustus to span the Lys torrent, stands as one of Italy's best-preserved Roman arches. Anchored directly into rock on both banks, it rises 25 metres above the water, with a single span of 35 metres. At its base, you can still see the slots cut into the rock for the wooden beams that served as scaffolding during construction. The bridge was part of the Route des Gaules linking Eporedia (Ivrea) to Augusta Praetoria (Aosta), and on to the Great and Little Saint Bernard passes. In 1836 it ceased to be used for traffic, but it survived intact even the Allied bombing of 25 May 1944, which destroyed much of the village. Legend, celebrated every year at the historic carnival, tells how Saint Martin negotiated its construction with the devil: he threw a piece of bread to the far side, and a starving dog raced across first, carrying off the promised soul with it. The devil, outwitted, vanished into the waters of the Lys.

45 min
2

Fort Bard rises from a rock outcrop commanding the valley's narrowest point. The first defensive works date to the 10th century (first documented in 1034, belonging to Viscount Boso of Aosta). In May 1800, the fort - defended by just 400 soldiers under Captain Stockard von Bernkopf - held up Napoleon's reserve army for two weeks, forcing 40,000 men and 50 cannons to bypass the fortress under cover of night, their cannon wheels wrapped in hay to muffle the sound. Napoleon ordered the fort razed after his victory. King Charles Albert of Sardinia had it rebuilt between 1830 and 1838, designed by engineer Francesco Antonio Olivero. Decommissioned in 1975, purchased by the Region in 1990, and renovated over 10 years at a cost of 45 million euros, it reopened in 2006 as a cultural hub. The Museum of the Alps (in the Carlo Alberto building) offers an interactive journey through Alpine geography, cultures, and glaciers from Slovenia to France. The medieval village of Bard at its foot is accessible by panoramic elevator.

120 min
3

Three kilometres from the fort at Donnas, the Route des Gaules is cut directly into the rock for 221 metres, topped by an arched passage 4 metres thick, 4 metres high, and nearly 3 metres wide. It's one of the most spectacular testimonies to Roman road engineering in the Alps, still visible in its original state.

45 min

Activities

Hike in the Mont Avic Natural Park, created in 1989, accessed via Champdepraz: allow a day trip there and back from Pont-Saint-Martin. More than 30 lakes and peat bogs, vast forests of mountain pine, wild landscapes. One of the best-preserved natural areas in the Aosta Valley.

240 min

Historic Carnival of Pont-Saint-Martin (February/March): over 1,000 costumed performers recreate the town's Roman history. On Shrove Tuesday night, an effigy of the devil is burned beneath the Roman bridge arch in a spectacular pyrotechnic display.

180 min

Champoluc

Suggested base : Champoluc
★★★☆☆
1

Graines Castle at Brusson (1,375 m) ranks among the Aosta Valley's oldest medieval fortresses, built in the 11th century on a rocky spur at the mouth of Freudière Valley. Classified as a “primitive” castle by Valdostan architectural historians, it consists of a high crenellated tower on a quadrangular plan, a chapel, and walls following the cliff's contours. Its position commanded visual control of the Ayas valley floor and access to the upper Gressoney valley. Around 1200, the Graines family's lands passed to the powerful House of Challant, viscounts of Aosta.

60 min
2

The Ayas valley was colonised by the Walser from the 13th century onward: these mountain people of Germanic origin from the Upper Valais crossed the Alpine passes and settled in the upper valley, leaving their mark in the villages of Mascognaz and Saint-Jacques-des-Allemands (Canton of the Germans). Their rascard—larch granaries on stilts with stone-slab roofs—are still visible at Antagnod and Champoluc. By the 19th century, the Walser language had vanished from this valley, supplanted by Valdostan Franco-Provençal patois.

60 min
3

The castle of Verrès (14th century), at the entrance to the valley, stands on an 80-metre rock overlooking the confluence of the Evançon torrent and the Dora Baltea: allow a day trip there and back from Champoluc. A fortress with a rectangular plan and four domed corner towers, built around 1390 by Ibleto de Challant, it now houses a museum devoted to the medieval history of the Aosta Valley.

60 min

Chatillon

Suggested base : Chatillon
★★★☆☆
1

Ussel Castle, built by Ébal II de Challant in 1343 (dendrochronology-confirmed date) on a serpentinite promontory above Châtillon, marks the Aosta Valley's first monobloc castle - an architectural rupture between castles articulated in multiple volumes (like Fénis) and austere single-mass fortresses (like Verrès). Rectangular in form with a summit band of small blind arches, paired windows with floral decoration, and two cylindrical towers to the south, it passed through Challant hands until François de Challant's death in 1470, then served as prison and barracks before abandonment. In 1983, Baron Marcel Bich - inventor of the Bic pen and Châtillon native - purchased and donated it to the Region, which restored it as an exhibition space. From the terraces: sweeping views across the main valley to the Valtournenche entrance.

75 min
2

Saint-Vincent, 2 km from Châtillon, was a renowned spa town from the 18th century onwards, but its thermal baths are now closed. Its casino - the Casino de la Vallée - was for decades one of the main sources of funding for the Aosta Valley regional budget. The village keeps the fresco dedicated to Saint Maurice on the market square with the motto “en adversité, bon coeur”, added to the coat of arms in 1565 by Duke Emmanuel Philibert.

45 min
3

Fénis Castle, 10 km west of Châtillon, is the Aosta Valley's most photographed castle. Built by Aimone de Challant in the 14th century, it features double concentric walls, semi-circular towers, and an inner courtyard decorated with frescoes depicting Saint George slaying the dragon and a court scene with philosophers holding phylacteries - the work of Giacomo Jaquerio, circa 1415-1420.

75 min

Activities

Wine tasting along the Aosta Valley Wine Route: terraced vineyards at Chambave, Nus, and Montjovet produce local DOC wines like Chambave Muscat, Nus Malvoisie, and Torrette made from Petit Rouge. These “heroic” vineyards clinging to Alpine slopes between 400 and 900 metres have been cultivated by hand for centuries.

120 min

Hike the Cammino Balteo trail from Châtillon: discovery route through the Aosta Valley's natural and cultural heritage at mid and low elevations, accessible year-round.

180 min

Breuil Cervinia

Suggested base : Breuil Cervinia
★★★★☆
1

The Matterhorn (4,478 m) is one of the most recognisable mountains in the world thanks to its almost perfect pyramid shape, the result of four glacial cirques cutting into it. From Breuil - renamed Cervinia in 1936-1937 at the urging of senator Dino Lora Totino to establish the resort's national identity -, the Lion ridge rises towards the summit. On 17 July 1865, the Valtournenche guide Jean-Antoine Carrel opened the first Italian route on the Matterhorn along this ridge, three days after the Englishman Edward Whymper's feat from Zermatt. Whymper's descent cost the lives of four of his companions. In 1890, Carrel died on the mountain he had conquered, caught by a storm on the way back from a climb after bringing his clients to safety.

60 min
2

Cervinia's Blue Lake, a 45-minute walk from the village, offers the Matterhorn's reflection in turquoise waters. This glacial cirque lake at 2,153 metres is surrounded by a mineral amphitheatre whose hues shift from turquoise to cobalt depending on the hour. It's the starting point for trails to the Oriondé (Duca degli Abruzzi, 2,802 m) and Carrel (3,830 m) refuges - the two stages of the Matterhorn ascent by the Italian route.

90 min
3

The Matterhorn Alpine Crossing, inaugurated July 1, 2023, links Breuil-Cervinia to Zermatt by cable car for the first time without skis, crossing the Alps' highest border crossing. The Plateau Rosa (3,480 m) to Klein Matterhorn (3,883 m) section crosses the Italian-Swiss frontier above the glaciers, with direct views of the Matterhorn from both flanks.

120 min

Activities

Hike to Oriondé Refuge (Duca degli Abruzzi, 2,802 m) from Breuil: ascend the route of the first alpinists, pass the Carrel cross, view the Matterhorn's south face. Round trip approximately 5-6 hours.

300 min

In summer, ride the cable car to Plateau Rosa (3,480 m): panoramas of Matterhorn and Monte Rosa glaciers, summer ski slope, walking access to the seracs. In winter, international ski domain linked to Zermatt.

180 min

Aosta

Suggested base : Aosta
★★★★☆
1

Aosta (Augusta Praetoria Salassorum) was founded in 25 BC by the Romans following the final defeat of the Salassi people. The city preserves its Roman grid plan intact: today's streets still follow the ancient cardines and decumani orientation. The Arch of Augustus, erected immediately after the foundation to honour the alliance with the empire, stands 11.40 metres high with a single 9-metre-wide arch, flanked by ten Corinthian half-columns on a Doric entablature. In the Middle Ages, a crucifix was placed there and the arch took the name Saint-Voût (from the Latin votum) - the original 15th-century crucifix is now preserved in the Cathedral Treasury Museum.

30 min
2

The Porta Praetoria, built in 25 BC, was the Roman city's main eastern entrance. It comprises two parallel arches, each with three openings - the central arch for chariots, the two lateral ones for pedestrians. The outer façade partially preserves its original travertine marble facing, revealed during 2012 excavations 3 metres below the current level. The adjacent Roman theatre, whose scenic façade reaches 22 metres, the underground cryptoporticus with a triple gallery (formerly the Romans' medieval market), and the 724 × 522-metre city wall complete the archaeological circuit.

75 min
3

The Collegiate Church of Sant'Orso, founded on a 4th-5th century paleo-Christian basilica, preserves a Romanesque cloister (1460) with grey marble columns decorated with historiated capitals among northern Italy's most remarkable. Ottonian frescoes from the 11th century adorn the church vaults, depicting Gospel scenes in an expressive style rare at this altitude. The neighbouring cathedral (12th century, remodelled 15th-16th centuries) houses in its choir two floor mosaics from the 12th and 14th centuries representing the months of the year, fantastic animals, the Tigris and Euphrates.

75 min
4

The megalithic area of Saint-Martin-de-Corléans, west of Aosta, is one of the Alps' most important prehistoric sites. Used from the fifth millennium BC, it has yielded anthropomorphic stelae remarkably similar to those of Petit Chasseur at Sion (Valais), testifying to trans-Alpine cultural links well before Roman times.

60 min

Cogne

Suggested base : Aosta · 15 km
★★★★☆
1

Gran Paradiso National Park, inaugurated December 3, 1922, is Italy's first national park. Its history is inseparable from the Alpine ibex: in 1856, King Victor Emmanuel II declared these mountains a Royal Hunting Reserve, saving the species from extinction - only a few hundred remained. In 1919, his son Victor Emmanuel III offered the 2,100 hectares to the State on condition that a national park be created. In 1945, after World War II reduced the ibex population to 416 individuals, commissioner Renzo Videsott obtained autonomous park management in 1947, enabling gradual recovery. The park today shelters nearly 3,000 ibex - Europe's largest population - along with 8,000 chamois, marmots, lynx, and wolves.

60 min
2

The Cogne valley is the main Valdostan access to the park. At Valnontey, the Paradisia alpine botanical garden (mid-June to mid-September) assembles Alpine flora species, medicinal plants, and lichens. Its name comes from the white lily Paradisea liliastrum, endemic to the park. Cogne's historic centre perpetuates the tombolo tradition—bobbin lace—transmitted for centuries.

60 min
3

The Lillaz waterfalls, a 10-minute walk from the eponymous hamlet, drop 150 metres in three successive rocky leaps on the Urtier torrent. Rifugio Vittorio Sella (2,584 m), originally a royal hunting lodge built in 1861 by order of the king, was converted to a mountain refuge in 1922 at the park's opening.

60 min

Activities

Hike from Valnontey to Rifugio Vittorio Sella (2,584 m), the park's heart: virtually guaranteed encounters with ibex (males with long curved horns averaging 92 cm) and chamois. Return by the same route.

360 min

Short hike to Lillaz waterfalls and Valnontey pastures: wildlife observation in late afternoon, flowering alpine meadows, views of the Grivola (3,969 m) and its glacier.

180 min

Courmayeur

Suggested base : Courmayeur
★★★★☆
1

The Great Saint Bernard Pass (2,473 m), the third-highest road pass in Switzerland, lies well away from Courmayeur, via Aosta: allow a day trip there and back from Courmayeur. It has been used since Antiquity - the Romans built a road here and a temple dedicated to Jupiter Poeninus, whose excavation has yielded more than 40 bronze votive offerings. The hospice was founded in 1050 by Bernard of Aosta, archdeacon of the cathedral, to shelter and protect travellers crossing the Alps - a mission uninterrupted for nearly a thousand years. In May 1800, Napoleon crossed the pass with 40,000 men, 5,000 horses and 50 cannons during his second Italian campaign; the episode was immortalised in the painting by Jacques-Louis David. The tomb of General Desaix, killed at Marengo a few weeks later, is on the stairs of the hospice.

90 min
2

Saint Bernard dogs were bred at the hospice from the 17th century onward, first as guard dogs, then to accompany the canons on daily patrols searching for travellers in distress in the snow. The breed was selected from dogs probably gifted by Valais families in the 1660s-1670s - first mentioned in 1709. Breeding has been entrusted since 2005 to the Barry Foundation in Martigny. Each summer, a dozen dogs return to the pass where visitors can accompany them on hikes. The hospice museum traces the pass's history from ancient cults to contemporary hospitality.

60 min

Monte Bianco Skyway Experience

Courmayeur

Suggested base : Courmayeur
★★★★☆
1

The Little Saint Bernard Pass (2,188 m), open in summer, links Courmayeur to Bourg-Saint-Maurice in Savoie; the road climbs via Pré-Saint-Didier and La Thuile, allow a day trip there and back from Courmayeur. It was one of the main Roman roads to Gaul (Alpis Graia). The Little Saint Bernard hospice, founded in the 11th century by Bernard of Aosta at the same time as the Great Saint Bernard hospice, has an alpine botanical garden created by Abbé Chanoux at the end of the 19th century, with more than 500 alpine species. A circle of pre-Roman standing stones (Cromlech), similar to Stonehenge but on a smaller scale, lies beside the hospice.

75 min
2

La Thuile, a village at 1,441 metres on the road to the Little St Bernard Pass, had a silver-bearing lead mine, worked from the 16th century, until 1967. Its Franco-Italian ski area is linked to La Rosière in Savoie. From the village, a trail leads to the Rutor waterfalls, fed by the glacier of the same name.

45 min
3

Morgex and La Salle, in the Valdigne, produce Blanc de Morgex et de la Salle DOC, from mountain vineyards grown between 900 and 1,300 m above sea level. The Prié Blanc grape is trained on low pergolas on glacial terraces - one of the few pre-phylloxera vineyards in Europe, spared by the parasite thanks to the altitude. The Cave Mont Blanc de Morgex et La Salle offers tastings and vineyard tours.

60 min

Activities

Pré-Saint-Didier thermal baths, near Courmayeur: outdoor pools at 37°C with views of the snow-capped peaks of Mont Blanc, treatments in the rock, waterfalls and open-air pools. The sulphur springs have been used since the 16th century.

180 min

Departure toward France (Mont Blanc tunnel, 11.6 km, open 24/7) or Switzerland (Great Saint Bernard pass or tunnel, depending on season). Courmayeur is 2 hours from Turin, 1.5 hours from Geneva, 3 hours from Milan.

60 min
