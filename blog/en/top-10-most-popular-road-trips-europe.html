1

France · Burgundy · 5 days · 15 stops · 525 km

The 15 most beautiful villages in Burgundy

People come to Burgundy for two things that go together: stone and wine. Listed villages, Romanesque abbeys, and the Route des Grands Crus linking the most famous wine climats in the world.

The route crosses the region from north to south, from Chablis to Cluny. Along the way: the UNESCO-listed basilica of Vézelay, the polychrome roofs of the Hospices de Beaune, the village of Noyers-sur-Serein with its 78 listed monuments, and Cluny Abbey, the largest church in Christendom until St Peter's in Rome was rebuilt. Between each stop, centuries-old cellars and hills of chardonnay and pinot noir.

2

Greece · Euboea · 6 days · 5 stops · 368 km

Euboea circuit: ancient springs and villages

Euboea is the large Greek island that classic tours overlook. You come here for a Greece without the crowds, between ancient springs and mountain villages.

The route starts at Edipsos and its 80 thermal springs, used since Aristotle and the Roman generals. It crosses Chalcis and its strait, whose currents reverse every six hours, climbs to Eretria, an ancient city destroyed by the Persians, and ends at Karystos, where columns of green marble once waited to be shipped to Rome. Above, on Mount Ochi, stand the Drakospita, megalithic houses no one has yet explained.

3

France · Occitania · 8 days · 13 stops · 1,693 km

The 13 most beautiful villages in Occitania

This is where you come for hilltop villages and gorges. Thirteen of France's Most Beautiful Villages on a single route, from the Tarn to the Pyrenees.

You'll find Cordes-sur-Ciel suspended in the clouds, Saint-Cirq-Lapopie clinging to a cliff 100 metres above the Lot, Larressingle the smallest fortified village in France, and the Templar walls of La Couvertoirade on the Larzac plateau. Between the villages, the Tarn gorges by canoe, Pyrenean spa towns, and three UNESCO sites: Saint-Bertrand-de-Comminges, Saint-Guilhem-le-Désert and the Pont du Gard.

4

Italy · Tuscany · 4 days · 10 stops · 348 km

The most beautiful villages of the Grosseto province

This is the Tuscany few travellers dare: the deep Maremma, far from the postcard cypresses. You come for tuff villages and hot springs out in the open.

The circuit links Massa Marittima and its Gothic cathedral, the trio of Pitigliano-Sorano-Sovana in tuff country with its Etruscan Vie Cave, and the Saturnia falls, where water gushes out at 37 °C, free all year round. It then drops to Porto Ercole and its Spanish forts, where Caravaggio died, before ending on the island of Giglio, granite and scrub in the middle of the sea.

5

Italy · Emilia-Romagna · 5 days · 13 stops · 301 km

The 10 natural sites around Bologna

Bologna is known for its porticoes and its food. This itinerary looks for something else: the secret nature of its hinterland, a few kilometres from the red city.

The route runs from the wetlands of the Po plain to the Dardagna falls, on the slopes of Corno alle Scale. You'll find Brisighella and its three pinnacles of pink gypsum, the Rocchetta Mattei, a Moorish castle lost in the Apennines, the heavy silence of Monte Sole, and glacial lakes at altitude.

6

Sweden · 10 days · 10 stops · 675 km

The Great South: from Malmö to Stockholm through the islands

This road trip links Malmö to Stockholm along the Baltic, crossing two islands of opposite character. You come for Viking and medieval history, right by the water.

The route passes through Ystad and its megalithic circle of Ales Stenar, raised on a cliff facing the sea. It crosses Öland, flat and windswept, UNESCO-listed for its limestone steppes, then Gotland and Visby, whose 3.4 km of medieval walls are almost intact. Between the two, Karlskrona and its baroque naval base, Kalmar and its Renaissance castle, then Stockholm and its Vasa museum.

7

Austria · 12 days · 15 stops · 1,190 km

Austria in 12 days: Wachau, the Alps and Salzburg

Twelve days to see Austria on a grand scale. You come for the contrast: the Danube vineyards, the Tyrolean glaciers, and the baroque of Salzburg.

The route starts in Vienna, follows the UNESCO-listed Wachau valley down the Danube, between the ruins of Dürnstein and Melk Abbey. It then climbs the Grossglockner road, 48 km and 36 hairpins, skirts the turquoise lakes of Tyrol, and rises to the Zugspitze and the Formarinsee. Innsbruck links its Habsburg old town to 2,256 metres of altitude in thirty minutes. Salzburg closes the loop on Mozart.

8

England · 10 days · 10 stops · 598 km

East Anglia: Norfolk, Suffolk and Cambridge

You come to East Anglia for forgotten England: wool villages, endless marshes and the colleges of Cambridge, well off the tourist trail.

The road trip starts at the Cambridge colleges and the Wren Library, passes the ruins of Bury St Edmunds Abbey, birthplace of the Magna Carta, and Lavenham, the best-preserved medieval village in England. It reaches the Norfolk coast to watch the seals of Blakeney Point, then the Broads, and ends before Ely Cathedral, standing like a ship above the marshes.

9

Spain · Galicia · 10 days · 10 stops · 550 km

Galicia: the full tour in 10 days

Galicia is green, Atlantic Spain, the opposite of the southern clichés. You come for the pilgrimage, the seafood and a viticulture clinging to the cliffs.

The loop starts at Santiago de Compostela and its cathedral, a Christian sanctuary since the 9th century. It drops to A Coruña and its Tower of Hercules, the only Roman lighthouse still in service, crosses the Costa da Morte to Cape Finisterre, dives into the wine-growing Rías Baixas, then climbs back through the canyons of the Ribeira Sacra to Lugo and its Roman wall.

10

Denmark · 13 days · 13 stops · 1,214 km

Denmark: from Copenhagen to Skagen

This grand tour of Denmark surprises at every stop. You come for the castles, the Vikings, the design, and two seas that collide at the top of the country.

From Copenhagen and its canals, the route reaches the castles of Frederiksborg and Kronborg, Shakespeare's Hamlet, then the five Viking ships of Roskilde and Odense. In Aarhus, Eliasson's panoramic rainbow crowns the ARoS museum. Further north, the Viking cemetery of Lindholm Høje, Skagen and its painters, and the tip of Grenen where the North Sea and the Baltic meet.

Frequently asked questions

How is this ranking established?+

It reflects the ten most viewed itineraries on OneRoadTrip. The order follows the number of views, from most to least viewed.

Are the itineraries free?+

Yes. Every itinerary is free, fully viewable and customisable: you can change the stops, the order and the duration, and add your own accommodation.

When is the best time to go?+

For most of these destinations, late spring and early autumn offer the best balance of weather and crowds. Each itinerary states its own ideal window.

Do you need a car for these road trips?+

A car remains the most flexible option. Some routes, such as Sweden or Denmark, also work well with combined transport (trains, buses and ferries).
